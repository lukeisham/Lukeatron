# Casper (Acuity Insights) — US

**Reference test:** Casper (Acuity Insights) — US medical/dental/veterinary school admissions
**Format:** Text-based or video scenario followed by 3 open-ended constructed-response questions. No multiple choice.
**Answer style:** Free-text typed responses, scored on professionalism, empathy, communication, ethics, and problem-solving.
**Scoring:** Human raters score each response on a 1–9 scale across multiple competencies. No single "correct" answer; quality of reasoning and demonstration of non-cognitive skills matters.

## Casper Scenario — Prescription Error Disclosure

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Stimulus

You are a fourth-year medical student on your internal medicine rotation. During morning rounds, you notice that a patient on the ward has been receiving a medication at double the intended dose for the past two days. The error was made by Dr. Chen, a respected senior resident who has been mentoring you closely and wrote you a strong letter of recommendation for your residency applications. The patient has experienced mild side effects (nausea, dizziness) but is clinically stable. Dr. Chen has not yet noticed the error. Your attending physician is about to finalise the discharge summary.

### Questions

1. **What would you do in this situation?** Describe the specific steps you would take and explain your reasoning.

2. **What factors would you consider when deciding how to address this with Dr. Chen?** Consider both your professional obligations and your personal relationship.

3. **If Dr. Chen asks you not to document the error because "the patient is fine and it will just create paperwork and liability issues," how would you respond?** Explain your reasoning.

### Competencies Assessed

| Competency | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **Ethics & Integrity** | Balancing loyalty to mentor vs. duty to patient safety and honest documentation |
| **Communication** | How the candidate would approach a difficult conversation with a senior colleague |
| **Professionalism** | Understanding of medical error disclosure standards and chain of responsibility |
| **Empathy** | Consideration for the patient, the mentor, and the broader care team |
| **Problem-Solving** | Practical steps to correct the error while preserving working relationships |

### Clue

Patient safety is non-negotiable, but how you communicate matters as much as what you decide. Think about timing, tone, and who needs to know what.

### Explainer

Strong responses typically: (1) prioritise patient safety by ensuring the error is corrected immediately and the discharge summary is accurate; (2) approach Dr. Chen privately, framing the conversation as collaborative ("I noticed something I wanted to run by you") rather than accusatory; (3) demonstrate understanding that error disclosure is a professional obligation, not optional; (4) acknowledge the mentorship dynamic while maintaining ethical boundaries; (5) recognise that near-misses and errors are learning opportunities, not just liability events. Weak responses either avoid the issue entirely or confront Dr. Chen publicly in a way that damages the relationship without improving patient safety.
