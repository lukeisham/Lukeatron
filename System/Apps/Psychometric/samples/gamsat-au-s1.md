# GAMSAT — Section 1: Reasoning in Humanities and Social Sciences — AU

**Reference test:** GAMSAT — Section 1 (Australian medical school admissions)
**Format:** 62 questions, 100 minutes. Passages drawn from humanities and social sciences: prose (fiction and non-fiction), poetry, cartoons, diagrams, tables, and philosophical texts. Questions test comprehension, interpretation, inference, and critical analysis.
**Answer style:** Single correct answer (A–D). Binary scoring.
**Distinctive feature:** Broader variety of stimulus types than MCAT CARS, including poetry, visual material, and highly literary prose.

## GAMSAT S1 Passage — Poetry Analysis

**Difficulty:** Hard
**Type:** Poetry interpretation

### Stimulus

*from* **"The Peace of Wild Things"** — *adapted for GAMSAT-style analysis*

When despair for the world grows in me
and I wake in the night at the least sound
in fear of what my life and my children's lives may be,
I go and lie down where the wood drake
rests in his beauty on the water, and the great heron feeds.
I come into the peace of wild things
who do not tax their lives with forethought
of grief. I come into the presence of still water.
And I feel above me the day-blind stars
waiting with their light. For a time
I rest in the grace of the world, and am free.

### Questions

**1.** The speaker's primary motivation for seeking out "wild things" is best described as:

- **A:** A desire to study and understand the natural world.
- **B:** An escape from the burden of anticipatory anxiety about the future.
- **C:** A longing to return to a simpler, pre-technological mode of existence.
- **D:** A wish to isolate himself permanently from human society.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:** The poem explicitly contrasts the speaker's mental state — "despair," "fear of what my life and my children's lives may be" — with the wild creatures who "do not tax their lives with forethought of grief." The speaker seeks relief from *anticipatory* anxiety (fear of what *may* be), not from society or technology generally. B captures this specific contrast. A is too cognitive/scientific. C and D overgeneralise.

---

**2.** The phrase "day-blind stars" (line 9) functions in the poem primarily to:

- **A:** Provide a factual astronomical observation about stellar visibility.
- **B:** Suggest that the natural world operates on a scale indifferent to human perception.
- **C:** Imply that the speaker is visiting the natural setting during daylight hours.
- **D:** Introduce a note of pessimism by suggesting the stars are weakened during the day.

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:** "Day-blind stars" suggests the stars are still present, still "waiting with their light," even though the speaker cannot see them. This conveys a sense of natural constancy and patience that exists independently of human awareness — a world that does not need to be perceived to be steady. C is too literal. D misreads the tone: the stars are "waiting," which is steady and reassuring, not weakened.

---

**3.** The speaker describes experiencing freedom ("and am free") specifically through:

- **A:** Physical isolation from other humans.
- **B:** Acceptance of mortality and the limits of human control.
- **C:** Temporary relief from the psychological weight of concern for the future.
- **D:** A philosophical epiphany about humanity's place in the cosmos.

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:** The freedom described is temporary ("For a time") and follows directly from resting "in the grace of the world." The mechanism of this relief is specified earlier: wild things "do not tax their lives with forethought of grief," and the speaker attains peace by entering their domain, temporarily shedding the specifically human burden of worrying about what has not yet happened. C precisely identifies this. D overstates the metaphysical dimension. A misses the psychological mechanism.

---

**4.** Which of the following best describes the relationship between the poem's form and its content?

- **A:** The irregular line lengths mirror the speaker's chaotic emotional state.
- **B:** The absence of rhyme reflects the disorder of the natural world.
- **C:** The extended sentence structure, flowing across line breaks, enacts the speaker's movement from anxiety toward calm.
- **D:** The use of the first person isolates the reader from the speaker's experience.

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:** GAMSAT S1 frequently asks about the relationship between form and content. The poem uses enjambment and a single extended syntactic movement — the opening clause "When despair..." is not resolved until "I go and lie down," and the poem flows continuously. This formal feature mirrors the speaker's psychological journey from wakeful anxiety to rest. C identifies this. A would be plausible if the poem were syntactically fractured, but it is fluid. B is wrong — the natural world is portrayed as orderly. D is opposite — first person invites identification.
