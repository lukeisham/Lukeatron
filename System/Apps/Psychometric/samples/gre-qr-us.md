# GRE — Quantitative Reasoning — US

**Reference test:** GRE — Quantitative Reasoning section (US graduate school admissions)
**Format:** Two scored sections, 27 questions total, 47 minutes total. Mix of Quantitative Comparison, Multiple Choice (single and multiple answer), and Numeric Entry. On-screen calculator provided.
**Answer style:** Varies by question type. Multiple choice: single correct answer. Quantitative comparison: A (Quantity A is greater), B (Quantity B is greater), C (equal), D (cannot be determined).
**Distinctive feature:** Quantitative Comparison questions are unique to GRE — they ask whether one quantity is greater than another, not for a numeric value. These represent roughly one-third of the section.

---

## GRE QR — Quantitative Comparison

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Format:** Quantitative Comparison (A / B / C / D)

### Stimulus

**Quantity A:** The area of a circle with circumference 12π

**Quantity B:** The area of a square with diagonal length 12

### Question

Which quantity is greater?

- **A:** Quantity A is greater.
- **B:** Quantity B is greater.
- **C:** The two quantities are equal.
- **D:** The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

### Correct Answer

**A**

### Calculation

**Quantity A — Area of the circle:**
- Circumference = 2πr = 12π → r = 6
- Area = πr² = π(36) = 36π ≈ 113.10

**Quantity B — Area of the square:**
- Diagonal = 12
- For a square, diagonal = s√2 (where s = side length)
- s = 12/√2 = 12√2/2 = 6√2
- Area = s² = (6√2)² = 36 × 2 = 72

**Comparison:** 36π > 72 (since π ≈ 3.14, 36 × 3.14 ≈ 113 > 72)

### Clue

Both quantities are expressed in terms of a single defining measurement (circumference for the circle, diagonal for the square). Express each area in terms of that measurement and compare. Be careful: a circle and square with equal "spanning" measurements do not have equal areas.

### Explainer

This is a classic GRE Quantitative Comparison. The test rewards recognising that you don't need to calculate exact decimal values — knowing that π > 3 is sufficient to determine that 36π > 108 > 72. Common traps: (1) Assuming a circle and square with visually similar dimensions have similar areas; (2) Forgetting the diagonal formula for a square (s√2); (3) Spending time calculating precise decimal values when an inequality test suffices. GRE strategy: in quantitative comparisons, test if one quantity is provably larger, smaller, or equal. If a single inequality relationship holds for all possible cases, you have your answer.
