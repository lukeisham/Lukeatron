# STAT (Special Tertiary Admissions Test) — Multiple Choice — AU

**Reference test:** STAT Multiple Choice (Australian tertiary admissions — mature-age and alternative entry)
**Format:** 70 questions, 2 hours. Two components: Verbal/Critical Reasoning (35 questions) and Quantitative Reasoning (35 questions). Questions are standalone — no shared stimuli or passage sets.
**Answer style:** Single correct answer (A–D). Binary scoring.
**Distinctive feature:** STAT is designed for candidates who may not have recent secondary education. Questions test reasoning and comprehension rather than curriculum knowledge. Both verbal and quantitative components are included. The sample below covers one of each.

---

## STAT — Verbal/Critical Reasoning Item

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Stimulus

"In a democratic society, the freedom of the press is often described as the 'fourth estate' — an institution that serves as a check on the power of government by informing the public and scrutinising those in authority. However, this function depends on the press being both independent and trusted. When news organisations become closely aligned with political parties or commercial interests, their capacity to hold power to account is compromised. For this reason, media ownership concentration — where a small number of corporations control a large share of news outlets — poses a threat to the democratic role of the press."

### Question

According to the passage, the democratic function of the press is undermined when:

- **A:** The government attempts to regulate media content.
- **B:** News organisations become economically unviable.
- **C:** Media ownership is concentrated in the hands of a few corporations.
- **D:** Journalists are not adequately trained in investigative techniques.

### Correct Answer

**C**

### Explainer

The passage explicitly states: "For this reason, media ownership concentration... poses a threat to the democratic role of the press." C directly restates this. A is not discussed — government regulation is not mentioned as a threat to press independence. B is about economic viability, not about the mechanism described in the passage (alignment with political/commercial interests). D introduces a factor (training) not discussed in the passage. STAT verbal reasoning tests careful reading and the ability to identify what the passage explicitly states, not what might be true in general.

## STAT — Quantitative Reasoning Item

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Stimulus

A car's fuel consumption is 8.5 litres per 100 kilometres on highways and 11.2 litres per 100 kilometres in city driving. On a particular trip, the car travels 240 km on highways and 60 km in the city.

### Question

Approximately how many litres of fuel does the car consume on this trip?

- **A:** 27.1 L
- **B:** 29.5 L
- **C:** 31.6 L
- **D:** 33.2 L

### Correct Answer

**A (27.1 L)**

### Calculation

- Highway consumption: (240 km ÷ 100) × 8.5 L = 2.4 × 8.5 = 20.4 L
- City consumption: (60 km ÷ 100) × 11.2 L = 0.6 × 11.2 = 6.72 L
- Total: 20.4 + 6.72 = 27.12 ≈ 27.1 L

### Explainer

STAT quantitative reasoning tests practical numeracy: interpreting rates (litres per 100 km), proportional reasoning, and multi-step calculation. This question requires: (1) understanding that the consumption rate is "per 100 km" so the distance must be divided by 100; (2) calculating consumption separately for each driving condition; (3) summing for the total. Common error: averaging the two consumption rates (9.85 L/100 km) and multiplying by total distance (300 km ÷ 100 × 9.85 = 29.55 L), which ignores the unequal distances driven under each condition — that yields option B or C and is incorrect because the weighted average differs from the simple average.
