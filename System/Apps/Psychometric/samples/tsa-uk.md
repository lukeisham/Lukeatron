# TSA (Thinking Skills Assessment) — UK

**Reference test:** TSA — Thinking Skills Assessment (UK — Oxford, Cambridge, UCL admissions for various courses)
**Format:** 50 questions, 90 minutes. Divided into 25 Problem Solving questions and 25 Critical Thinking questions. The sample below is from the Critical Thinking section.
**Answer style:** Single correct answer (A–E). Binary scoring.
**Distinctive feature:** TSA Critical Thinking questions are similar in logic to LSAT LR but typically use shorter stimulus passages and are designed to be accessible to applicants who have not studied formal logic. Questions often identify flaws, assumptions, or evaluate the strength of reasoning.

## TSA — Critical Thinking: Identifying the Main Conclusion

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Stimulus

Many people argue that artificial intelligence will lead to mass unemployment, as machines replace human workers across numerous industries. However, historical evidence does not support this fear. The Industrial Revolution automated countless manual tasks, yet total employment actually increased over the following century because new categories of jobs emerged that no one could have predicted. There is no reason to believe that the AI revolution will be fundamentally different in this regard. The jobs that disappear will be replaced by new roles that we cannot currently envision. Predictions of permanent mass unemployment are therefore unwarranted.

### Question

Which of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument?

### Options

- **A:** Artificial intelligence will lead to mass unemployment.
- **B:** The Industrial Revolution automated countless manual tasks.
- **C:** Historical evidence does not support the fear that AI will cause mass unemployment.
- **D:** New categories of jobs emerged after the Industrial Revolution.
- **E:** Predictions of permanent mass unemployment are unwarranted.

### Correct Answer

**E**

### Clue

The main conclusion is the claim that the rest of the argument is trying to establish. It often appears at the beginning or end of the passage and is supported by reasons, rather than itself supporting another claim.

### Explainer

**E is the main conclusion.** The passage structure is:
- **Counter-claim:** "AI will lead to mass unemployment" (presented as what "many people argue")
- **Reason 1:** Historical evidence (Industrial Revolution — automation didn't cause permanent unemployment)
- **Reason 2:** No reason to think AI is different
- **Reason 3:** New jobs will emerge
- **Conclusion:** "Predictions of permanent mass unemployment are therefore unwarranted" (signalled by "therefore")

Option A is the view the argument is *rejecting*. B and D are premises (evidence supporting the conclusion). C is an intermediate conclusion — it is supported by B and D, but it itself supports E. The word "therefore" in the final sentence is the key structural signal identifying E as the ultimate conclusion.

This question type tests the ability to distinguish between premises, intermediate conclusions, and the main conclusion — a core TSA Critical Thinking skill.
