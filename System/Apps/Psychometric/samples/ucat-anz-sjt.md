## UCAT ANZ SJT Scenario — Rural Placement Resource Dispute

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Format:** Rank (Most → Least appropriate)

### Stimulus

You are a final-year medical student completing a rural GP placement in a small town with limited resources. The practice has only one treatment room with specialised equipment for wound care. Two patients need the room simultaneously: an elderly Indigenous man with a complex leg ulcer requiring immediate debridement, and a young child with a deep cut from farm equipment whose anxious mother has been waiting for 45 minutes. The practice nurse suggests treating the child first because "he's more distressed." You are the most senior student present, and the supervising GP is currently attending a home visit 20 minutes away.

### Question

Rank the following responses from **most to least** appropriate:

### Options

- **A:** Treat the child first because the mother has been waiting longer and is visibly distressed.
- **B:** Ask the practice nurse to begin preparing the child's wound while you start the elderly man's debridement, splitting your time between both patients.
- **C:** Call the supervising GP at the home visit to ask for guidance on prioritisation.
- **D:** Explain to the child's mother that the elderly man's condition is more clinically urgent, and ask for her understanding while you treat him first.

### Correct Answer

**D, C, B, A** (Most → Least appropriate)

### Clue

Clinical urgency should guide triage decisions, not wait time or visible distress. Consider how to communicate respectfully with patients while maintaining clinical priorities.

### Explainer

**D (Most appropriate):** Clinical triage dictates that the leg ulcer requiring debridement takes priority — delayed treatment risks infection and tissue damage. Explaining this clearly and respectfully to the mother demonstrates both clinical reasoning and patient-centred communication. Cultural sensitivity is also relevant when caring for Indigenous patients.

**C (Appropriate):** Consulting the supervising GP respects the limits of your competence as a student and ensures the decision has clinical oversight. However, a 20-minute delay may be clinically significant for the elderly patient's debridement, and the GP may not have full situational awareness.

**B (Less appropriate):** Splitting attention between two procedures risks compromising the quality of both, and as a student you may not be competent to manage two complex wound cases simultaneously. Delegation to the nurse is reasonable, but simultaneous management is overconfident.

**A (Least appropriate):** Prioritising based on wait time and visible distress rather than clinical urgency is a fundamental triage error. The child's cut, while traumatic, is less time-critical than a wound requiring debridement. This also fails to communicate with the mother about why clinical decisions are being made.
