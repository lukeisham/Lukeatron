**Answer style:** True / False / Can't Tell. Binary scoring.

## UCAT ANZ VR Passage — Indigenous Health Outcomes

**Difficulty:** Hard

### Stimulus

The life expectancy gap between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians remains one of the most persistent health equity challenges in the developed world. According to the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare, the gap stood at approximately 8 years for males and 7.5 years for females as of 2018, a reduction of about 2 years since 2005. Cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease account for the largest share of the mortality differential, with rates of diabetes among Indigenous adults approximately three times those of non-Indigenous adults.

The federal government's Closing the Gap framework, first established in 2008, set seven targets across health, education, and employment. By 2020, only two of the seven targets were on track to be met, and the target to close the life expectancy gap by 2031 was assessed as not on track. In 2020, a new National Agreement on Closing the Gap was signed, which for the first time included shared decision-making provisions and was developed in partnership with the Coalition of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peak Organisations. The new framework expanded to 17 targets, including specific measures for suicide prevention, access to disability services, and rates of Indigenous children in out-of-home care.

Research consistently identifies access to culturally safe healthcare as a critical determinant of health outcomes. A 2019 study published in the Medical Journal of Australia found that Indigenous patients who had access to Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations (ACCHOs) reported significantly higher levels of trust in their healthcare providers and were more likely to attend follow-up appointments than those accessing mainstream services. ACCHOs are primary healthcare services initiated and operated by local Aboriginal communities, and there are now over 140 such organisations operating across Australia.

### Questions

For each statement, select **True**, **False**, or **Can't Tell**.

**1.** The life expectancy gap between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians was approximately 10 years for males in 2005.

**Answer:** **True**

**Explanation:** The passage states the gap was approximately 8 years in 2018 "a reduction of about 2 years since 2005." Therefore, in 2005 the gap was approximately 8 + 2 = 10 years. The question specifies only males, and the passage states 8 years for males in 2018, so 10 years in 2005 follows logically.

---

**2.** The Closing the Gap framework established in 2008 included targets for reducing out-of-home care rates for Indigenous children.

**Answer:** **False**

**Explanation:** The passage states that the *original* 2008 framework set "seven targets across health, education, and employment." Out-of-home care rates are mentioned as targets in the *new* 2020 framework, which "expanded to 17 targets, including... rates of Indigenous children in out-of-home care." The statement refers to the 2008 framework, which did not include this target. The statement is therefore false.

---

**3.** Indigenous adults in Australia have a diabetes rate at least double that of non-Indigenous adults.

**Answer:** **True**

**Explanation:** The passage states "rates of diabetes among Indigenous adults approximately three times those of non-Indigenous adults." Three times is at least double, so the statement is true.

---

**4.** Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations have been shown to improve health outcomes for Indigenous patients compared to mainstream health services.

**Answer:** **Can't Tell**

**Explanation:** The passage states that patients accessing ACCHOs reported "higher levels of trust" and "were more likely to attend follow-up appointments." These are intermediate outcomes (trust, attendance), not final health outcomes (mortality, morbidity, condition management). The passage does not state that ACCHOs actually improve health outcomes — it only provides evidence of improved engagement with healthcare, which *may* lead to better outcomes but doesn't establish that it does. This is a classic UCAT VR trap: distinguishing between process measures and outcome measures.

---

### Clue

Pay close attention to the distinction between what the passage *directly states* and what it *implies or suggests but does not establish*. Process measures (trust, attendance) are not the same as outcome measures (actual health improvements).
