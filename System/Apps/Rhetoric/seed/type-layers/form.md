# Form

- Replacement — one thing stands in for another
  - Substitution — a word or phrase is swapped for a different one that takes its place
    - Renaming — swaps a name, term or word class for another
    - Recolouring — swaps a term for a kinder or harsher one
    - Persona — puts a person, voice or agent in place of something else
  - Metonymy — a thing is named by something closely associated with it
  - Synecdoche — a part stands for the whole, or the whole for a part
- Pairing — comparing or placing items side by side
  - Comparison — two things are set against each other so their likeness or difference shows
  - Juxtaposition — two things are placed next to each other with no explicit link, so the contrast speaks
- Sonic — sound-based patterning
  - Repetition — a word, phrase or structure recurs
    - Fixed Position — repeats a word at a set place in successive clauses
    - Close Repetition — repeats a word at once or with little between
    - Root Repetition — repeats a word's root or ending in different forms
    - Structural Repetition — repeats a grammatical shape, length or connective
    - Insistence — repeats a claim or question to press it home
  - Alliteration — nearby words begin with the same sound
  - Assonance — nearby words repeat the same vowel sound
- Addition & Omission — expanding or contracting
  - Addition — extra words or elements are added beyond what is strictly needed
    - Elaboration — adds an explanation, reason or division of what was said
    - Description — adds vivid detail that sets a thing before the senses
    - Amplification — adds words that restate or pile onto one idea
    - Insertion & Framing — adds a word or remark that flags, splits or tempers the statement
  - Omission — words or elements are left out and the audience supplies them
  - Escalation — successive elements build in size, force or importance
- Reversal — flipping the expected order or meaning
  - Inversion — the normal order of words or parts is turned around
  - Reversal — a statement or expectation is turned into its opposite
    - Irony — says one thing and means its opposite
    - Saying by Denying — affirms or raises a thing by denying or refusing it
    - Reversed Expectation — turns the course of a statement against what was expected
- Semantic Play — multiple meanings or ambiguity
  - Ambiguity — a word or phrase is left open to more than one meaning
    - Wordplay — plays openly on a word's two senses
    - Hidden Meaning — carries a second meaning beneath the surface one
    - Sense in Argument — shifts or fixes a term's sense within reasoning
- Argument Structure — the shape of the reasoning a device uses
  - Conditional Schema — if-then reasoning, in its valid and invalid forms
    - Valid Forms — the conclusion follows from the premises
    - Invalid Forms — the conclusion does not follow from the premises
    - Tentative Forms — an invalid shape offered openly as a hunch or likelihood
  - Causal Inference — reading a cause from order, co-occurrence or a single factor
    - Correlation & Sequence — reads a cause from things happening together or in order
    - Patterns & Chance — reads meaning into clusters, streaks and odds
    - Causes & Chains — names the cause, or the chain of effects that follows
  - Part–Whole Inference — moving between a whole, its parts and a sample of them
    - Sample to Whole — moves from a few cases to a general claim
    - Parts & Wholes — moves a property between a whole and its parts
    - Group & Member — moves between a group's traits and its members
    - Rule & Case — applies a general rule to a particular case
  - Appeal to a Standard — resting a claim on authority, tradition, nature, numbers, novelty or outcomes
    - Authority — measures a claim by who vouches for it
    - Age, Novelty & the Crowd — measures a claim by how old, new or widely held it is
    - Nature — measures a claim by what is natural or what a thing is for
    - Outcomes & Investment — measures a claim by what it brings or what has been spent
  - Appeal to Pressure — moving the audience by pity, threat or mockery in place of reasons
    - Pity & Sentiment — presses by stirring sympathy or longing
    - Threat & Stakes — presses by naming harm that will follow
    - Ridicule — presses by making a position laughable
  - Attack on the Source — rejecting a claim through the person, origin or manner of its speaker
    - Character & Motive — targets the speaker's character or reasons for holding the view
    - Origin & Association — targets where a claim came from or who it keeps company with
    - Consistency & Manner — targets how the speaker behaves or speaks
  - Option Framing — presenting, narrowing or denying the range of options
  - Assumed Premise — building the conclusion, a presupposition or a shifted standard into the argument
    - Built-in Conclusion — a premise or question already carries what is to be shown
    - Shifting Standards — the rule, bar or category is changed to fit the case
    - Hidden Bridge — an unstated premise carries the step from one kind of claim to another
  - Absence as Evidence — inferring from what is missing, unproven or unimaginable
  - Diversion — leaving the point at issue for another
- Speech Act — a device defined by the kind of utterance it is
  - Question — asking in order to make a point, not to get an answer
  - Exclamation — an outcry or an address to someone or something
  - Concession — yielding a point
  - Dismissal — brushing a claim or a person aside
  - Recollection & Example — bringing in the past or a specific case
- Sentence Shape — a device defined by how the sentence is built or how compact it is
  - Broken Construction — a sentence that changes course or leaves its grammar unfinished
  - Subordination — clauses ranked under a main clause
  - Numbered Points — points announced and ordered for the audience
  - Compact Saying — a brief, self-contained, pointed statement
