# Blueprint / schematic style
**Tags:** technical
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** no colour fills or gradients outside the blue/white (or inverted) register; no uniform single line weight — the four-tier hierarchy is the language; no dimension without its extension lines; no hatching crossing a boundary it shouldn't; never omit the title block or a stated scale; no text that isn't upright and left-to-right readable.
**Visual DNA:** Engineering blueprint — a deep-blue ground carrying crisp white/cyan technical line work, fully-specified dimension lines with arrowheads and measurements, angled section hatching, and a bottom-right title block inside a bordered sheet; everything is drawing, nothing is "art".
**Signature tells (must be visibly present):** 1) deep-blue (or inverted white) monochrome ground with white/cyan linework only, never a colour render 2) fully dimensioned lines — extension gaps, arrowhead-terminated dimension lines, and readable measurement numerals along every edge that needs one 3) a bordered sheet with a bottom-right title block, always present.
**Craft-rule carve-outs:** The four-tier line-weight hierarchy (object/hidden/centre/dimension, each a fixed `stroke-width`) is the defining tell of this style and overrides the generic edge-variation rule — weight is assigned by line FUNCTION, never by depth or adjacency, and two adjacent object-lines may legitimately share the same weight. This style also takes no global light direction, gradients, or contact shadows — it is a flat line drawing, not a rendered scene.
**Typical subject / composition:** Choose this style for orthographic, dimensioned, measurable engineering drawing — mechanical parts, architectural plans/elevations, exploded-parts schematics, piping/electrical diagrams — anywhere a real fabricator or reader needs measurements, not just spatial impression (use isometric-technical-diagram for that). Composition is a single flat orthographic view (plan, elevation, or section) laid inside a bordered sheet with margin, dimensions running along the outside edges, and the title block anchoring the bottom-right corner.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="blueprint-ground">` — solid deep-blue field filling the sheet
- `<g id="grid">` — faint background rule grid, spacing 40px on a 1080-wide canvas; a drafting aid, never a data element
- `<g id="sheet-border">` — the drawing frame/border with margin, at object-line weight
- `<g id="main-linework">` — the subject's visible/object outline in white/cyan (solid, heaviest weight)
- `<g id="hidden-lines">` — occluded edges (medium weight, short evenly-spaced dashes)
- `<g id="centre-lines">` — axes of symmetry / bolt-circle centres (light weight, long-dash-short-dash)
- `<g id="cut-hatch">` — 45° section-hatch pattern fill inside cut/section areas
- `<g id="dimensions">` — extension lines, dimension lines, arrowhead markers, measurement text (lightest weight)
- `<g id="title-block">` — bordered corner panel with drawing metadata
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Four-tier line hierarchy (weights given on a 1080-wide canvas), each a fixed `stroke-width` and `stroke-dasharray`: **object/visible** — heaviest, `stroke-width: 3`, solid (`stroke-dasharray: none`); **hidden** — medium, `stroke-width: 1.75`, short evenly-spaced dashes `stroke-dasharray: 6 4`; **centre** — light, `stroke-width: 1`, long-dash-short-dash alternating `stroke-dasharray: 18 4 4 4`; **dimension/extension** — lightest, `stroke-width: 0.75`, solid.
- Dimension convention, fully specified: each extension line starts with a **4px visible gap** off the object's edge, then runs past the dimension line by a **6px overrun**. The dimension line spans between the two extension lines and terminates at both ends in arrowheads, built as a reusable SVG `<marker>` — a narrow filled triangle roughly **10px long by 3px wide (≈3:1 length:width)**, `markerWidth="10" markerHeight="6" refX="10" refY="3" orient="auto"`, referenced via `marker-start`/`marker-end` on the dimension `<line>`. The measurement numeral sits centred on the dimension line's midpoint, offset just above it (clear of the stroke), reading left-to-right; for a vertical dimension, rotate the numeral 90° so it reads bottom-to-top when viewed from the right. When a dimension is too small to fit its arrows between the extension lines, flip the arrowheads to point outward from the gap instead of inward.
- Section hatching as a real `<pattern>`: 45° diagonal lines at roughly **8px pitch**, `stroke-width: 1`, defined with `patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` on a small tile (e.g. `width="8" height="8"`) containing one diagonal line, rotated into place with `patternTransform="rotate(45)"` on the `<pattern>` element (or bake the 45° into the tile's own line coordinates). Adjacent cut parts sharing a boundary get opposing-angle hatch (45° vs 135°) or an offset phase, so the joint between two parts reads clearly.
- Title block: anchored to the **bottom-right corner** of the sheet, sized roughly **300×140px** on a 1080-wide canvas. Outer border at object-line weight (`stroke-width: 3`), internal grid divisions at dimension-line weight (`stroke-width: 0.75`). Standard fields, left-to-right/top-to-bottom: drawing title, drawing number, scale, date, revision, drawn-by.
- Drawing border / frame: a rectangle inset from the canvas edge by a stated margin (e.g. **30px** on a 1080-wide canvas), drawn at object-line weight, enclosing the entire sheet including the title block.
**Palette:** classic cyanotype ground `#0b3d91` (deep Prussian blue) with linework in near-white `#e8f4ff` and pale cyan `#a9d4ff`; one thin red `#e53935` reserved for revision marks or emphasis. Historical note: the deep-blue-ground / white-line convention comes from the cyanotype process — a photographic contact print, not ink drawn on blue paper — which is why the ground is blue and the lines are white. A modern inverted variant (white/pale ground, dark navy or black linework) is an accepted alternative; keep the same four-tier weight hierarchy either way.
**Typography:** technical monospace or engineering-stencil-like stack (`"Courier New", "Consolas", monospace`) for dimension numerals and the title block, set uppercase, sized **14-16px** on a 1080-wide canvas for dimension text and **18-20px** for the drawing title field.

**Choosing this over its neighbours:** against `isometric-technical-diagram.md` — both are line-based engineering idioms, but blueprint is a flat orthographic (plan/elevation/section) view built to be MEASURED, carrying full dimension lines, hatching, and a title block; isometric is a 30° projected "how it fits together" view built to be UNDERSTOOD spatially, with flat-shaded faces and no dimensioning at all. If the brief needs measurements and a title block, this is the style; if it needs a single legible 3D-ish assembly view, use isometric instead.
