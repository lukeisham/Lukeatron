# Classic educational mnemonic aid style (e.g. "An Adventure with a Lion")
**Tags:** illustrative | comic
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** never bolt the mnemonic device on as a separate mark beside the subject, add any detail outside the letter's boundary, use an italic/script/distorted letterform, let outline and fill sit too close in luminance, or mix rendering weight/palette/anthropomorphic treatment across a set — titles and captions ARE permitted as separate text; it is only the letterform itself that must be fused into the subject's structure, never set alongside it.
**Visual DNA:** Mid-century phonics/reading-scheme illustration where the mnemonic letterform is a structural part of the subject's own form (e.g. a coiled snake's body becomes the S), rendered in bold friendly outline and a muted retro print palette.
**Typical subject / composition:** a single animal, object, or character built for an ORIGINAL letter/sound pairing — one centred figure per plate, captioned below, designed to be re-run consistently across a 26-letter set.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="paper-texture-background">` — warm off-white/cream ground with subtle print-grain
- `<g id="scene-background">` — simple flat setting (a few props, no clutter)
- `<g id="mnemonic-subject">` — the character/object embodying the concept (e.g. the lion)
- `<g id="mnemonic-device">` — the letter/shape integrated into the subject's own form (e.g. a letter shape traced by the mane, tail, or pose)
- `<g id="supporting-characters">` — secondary figures, if any
- `<g id="title-banner">`
- `<g id="caption-text">`
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Integrated mnemonic: the letterform is the primary structural element of the subject, not an outline laid over it — build it as a real shared path so a snake's coiled body IS the S, an apple's mass fills the counter of the A, both readings available at once. Apply the design test directly: if a child cannot identify the depicted object within a second or two, the mnemonic has failed — this is also why no decorative detail is allowed outside the letter's boundary, since it splits attention away from the fused form.
- Linework: single-weight black or dark ink outline at stroke-width 7–8px on a 1080-wide canvas, no brush modulation, crisp edges (no feathering); the outline colour is pure black or a dark tint of the fill colour, never an unrelated decorative colour; interior line is minimal — form reads as outline plus flat fill, with only a few marks for eyes and key features.
- Registration-mismatch look: offset two near-duplicate copies of the outline path by 2–3px on a 1080-wide canvas, the lower copy at 40–50% opacity, to fake a soft period print-registration mismatch instead of a razor-clean single line.
- Paper grain: a single low-opacity `feTurbulence` (`baseFrequency` 0.7–0.9, 2 octaves) fed into a subtle displacement or opacity map, applied once, only within `<g id="paper-texture-background">` — never reapplied elsewhere in the file.
- Two-tone shading, used only where depth is genuinely needed (e.g. a ball split by a horizon line): a hard-edged split into a darker lower flat fill and a lighter upper flat fill, no blending between them; if simulating halftone dots as a press artefact, say explicitly that is what is being done.
- Friendly proportions: head roughly 40–50% of total figure height (oversized relative to a naturalistic ~15–20%), simple rounded limbs, minimal facial detail — large round or oval eyes with a solid dark pupil set high, a simple curved smile or no mouth, a small dot or line for a nose if present, permanently cheerful expression, rounded corners throughout; fur/scales/texture suggested by outline alone or a few parallel lines, never rendered in full detail.
- Composition: single subject occupies roughly 60–75% of the frame on a 1080-wide canvas, centred and near-vertically symmetrical; caption centred below with clear space; no frame or box around the plate (a boxed border is a post-1970s tell).
**Palette:** vintage-print restrained, 2–4 spot colours per sheet — mustard yellow `#d99a2b`, teal `#2a7f8a`, brick red `#b3401e`, olive `#6b7a3c` — on a cream ground `#f2e9d2` or `#FFFEF9`, never pure white; period offset inks read as matte/slightly desaturated even at full strength, which is the era signature — day-glo and fuchsia are post-1960s and wrong here.
**Typography:** the mnemonic letterform itself is a rounded sans or slab serif at bold to semi-bold weight, soft terminals, generous x-height (some 1940s–50s sets use a semi-cursive school-hand form — single-storey a, two-storey g); title banner in a matching bold rounded/slab stack, e.g. `"Baloo 2", "Cooper Std", "Century Schoolbook", sans-serif`; caption text in a plain sans-serif for early readers, e.g. `"Century Gothic", Verdana, sans-serif`, lowercase or title case, centred, keyword optionally bold or slightly larger — never mix families between the letterform and the caption.
