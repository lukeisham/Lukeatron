# Hokusai-style instructional drawing / clear ukiyo-e composition
**Tags:** illustrative | line-art
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** Western chiaroscuro or any directional light-source shading, vanishing-point/atmospheric perspective or distance-based desaturation, smooth airbrush-style gradients, internal cross-hatching or tonal modelling within an outlined shape, a centred rule-of-thirds focal point, uniform contour weight with no taper, colours outside the historical pigment set, or reproducing a specific historical seal/artwork.
**Visual DNA:** A brush-derived, variable-width contour bounding strictly flat colour fields — a direct consequence of woodblock printing, where one key block carries the line and separate blocks register one flat colour each — composed in strong diagonals with a high horizon and large areas of empty paper (*ma*).
**Typical subject / composition:** Original landscape and nature scenes (waves, mountains, weather, travel) where a primary subject sits small in the background or margins while a secondary foreground element (often threatening or large in scale) dominates the frame; best for dramatic, flattened nature/travel imagery rather than portraiture.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="paper-ground">` — washi paper tone, visible as a colour field in its own right, no gradient
- `<g id="sky-water-bokashi">` — flat or bokashi-graded ground tone for sky/water
- `<g id="background-forms">` — stylised, flattened distant elements (mountains, distant boats), palette unchanged by distance
- `<g id="midground-elements">` — secondary flat colour shapes, positioned per the asymmetric composition rule below
- `<g id="foreground-subject">` — the dominant foreground mass, frequently cut by the canvas edge
- `<g id="pattern-fill-details">` — repeating decorative motifs (wave-crest, cloud-swirl, foliage) filling flat shapes
- `<g id="contour-line">` — the confident bounding line, stroked over the completed flat-colour fields (mirrors the key block being the structural reference that colour blocks register against; contour reads visually as topmost even though the key block is physically printed first)
- `<g id="seal-cartouche">` — small generic corner seal/title box, invented, never a specific historical seal or text
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Woodblock production logic (build in this order): lay down flat colour fields first (one shape per colour, no blending), then stroke the contour line over the top — this reproduces the *omohan* (key block) as structural reference and *irohan* (colour blocks) registering to it, and explains why colour never bleeds outside a line and why there is no internal tonal modelling.
- Weighted contour taper: build as a single filled tapering path (not double stroke passes) with weight tiers on a 1080-wide canvas — figures 6–8px, foreground water 4–6px, clouds 3–4px, distant mountains 2–3px — thickest under implied brush pressure at silhouette edges, thinnest on release strokes; allow the line to miss the fill edge by 1–2px in places (registration slip) rather than tracing it exactly.
- Bokashi gradient: a `linearGradient` with only 3–4 discrete stops (not a smooth ramp) — e.g. sky from `#1E3A8A` at the top to `#60A5FA` at the horizon — plus a light `feGaussianBlur` (stdDeviation 2–3) across the stop boundaries and a low-amplitude `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.02–0.05, low opacity displacement) to break mathematical smoothness; use the same technique for water, darker in the foreground band and paler toward the horizon.
- Decorative pattern fills: a repeating `<pattern>` (tile size roughly 40–80px on a 1080-wide canvas) of stylised wave-crest, cloud-swirl, or foliage motifs filling a flat shape in place of solid colour, used sparingly — one or two pattern-filled shapes per composition, not the whole scene.
- Asymmetric composition: horizon in the upper third (around y=300–400 on a 1080-wide canvas), primary subject small and positioned off-centre at background/margin scale, secondary foreground element large enough to be cut by the canvas edge on at least one side; diagonals dominate over horizontals/verticals.
**Palette:** Prussian blue (*bero-ai*) `#1E3A8A`–`#60A5FA`, traditional indigo (*ai*) `#2C5282` layered beneath it, *beni* safflower red `#DC2626`–`#F87171`, orpiment yellow `#FCD34D`, shell white `#FFFBF0` for snow/foam/highlights, washi paper ground `#E8DCC8`–`#D4C5B9`; palette is constrained to this set — inventing colours outside it is an anti-pattern.
**Typography:** none in the main image; if used at all, confine to a small corner cartouche or seal, generic in form and invented text, never reproducing specific historical characters or seals verbatim.
