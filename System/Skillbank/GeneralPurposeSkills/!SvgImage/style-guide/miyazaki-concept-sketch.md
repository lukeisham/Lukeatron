# Miyazaki-style concept sketch (observational clarity register)
**Tags:** illustrative | line-art
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** never erase or clean up the construction lines into a closed vector contour, never let colour spread past roughly a fifth of the drawing's area, and never render a full illustrated backdrop behind the paper ground.
**Visual DNA:** The pre-production imageboard register — construction scaffolding (perspective guides, centre lines, ellipse axes, ghosted alternate positions) is deliberately left in as a visible record of thinking, not erased once the "real" line is found; loose graphite/ink contour, one small selective colour accent, and hand-written mechanism notes sit over it.
**Signature tells (must be visibly present):** 1) visible construction scaffolding (perspective guides, centre lines, ellipse axes) left under the finished line rather than erased 2) exactly one small selective colour accent capped at ~20% of the drawing's area, everything else in graphite/ink line 3) hand-written mechanism-annotation notes with small hook-tipped arrows, not a typeset caption.
**Craft-rule carve-outs:** This register deliberately never resolves into a single closed Bézier contour — skill.md's Bézier-over-primitives rule still governs the *shape* of each searching offset-path segment, but the finished silhouette stays multi-line and slightly open at corners (see Deliberate overshoot below); do not clean the overlapping offset paths into one clean outline, since the unresolved scaffolding and searching line are the tells this style exists to show.
**Typical subject / composition:** exploratory studies made before the formal storyboard, to settle story, world and character aesthetic — one subject per sketch (a building, a machine, a creature, a costume, a gesture), often with a second ghosted pose or angle beside it, drawn to work out how the thing looks and moves rather than to finish it. Production context: imageboard → storyboard → layout → key frame → inbetween → paint/composite — this register is the first stage only, made for the team to think and talk with, not for the screen.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="sketch-paper-ground">` — flat cream/light-grey fill standing in for the paper (see Distinctive SVG techniques for optional texture)
- `<g id="construction-lines">` — perspective guides, centre lines, ellipse axes, ghosted alternate limb/wing positions, at 15-25% opacity
- `<g id="primary-linework">` — the confident observational contour drawn over the scaffolding, opaque (85-100%)
- `<g id="colour-accent">` — the single selective colour pass, opacity 40-70%, covering at most ~20% of the drawing's area
- `<g id="mechanism-annotation">` — small hand-drawn arrows/notes explaining how a part moves, folds, or works
- `<g id="margin-notes">` — informal handwritten marginalia (story/character notes, not mechanism-specific)
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Searching pencil line: build each contour as 2-3 near-coincident offset paths (offsets of 1-2px on a 1080-wide canvas) at 40-70% opacity each, so overlaps read as a hand hunting for the line rather than a single clean stroke; use `stroke-linecap="round"` throughout.
- Deliberate overshoot and open corners: at each corner, let the path continue 4-8px past the true meeting point on one arm, and leave the other arm 2-4px short — corners that don't quite close is the tell that separates this from a finished vector illustration.
- Optional graphite grain: a low-frequency `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency ~0.9, one octave) fed into a `feDisplacementMap` (scale 1-2) applied to the linework group gives a grainy, non-rasterized wobble; skip it entirely and rely on the offset-path technique above if a lighter build is wanted — either is valid, don't do both at full strength.
- Ghosted alternate position: repeat a limb, wing, or part's contour in a second position at 20-30% opacity with no fill, using the same offset-path technique, to show a range of motion under study.
- Hand-drawn mechanism annotation: a thin curved arrow (open path, no arrowhead marker — a small hand-drawn hook at the tip instead) plus 1-2 words of annotation text, placed in the margin beside the part it explains.
- Paper as background: the flat paper-tone fill (or, for a slightly richer ground, a subtle `<pattern>` of faint fibre flecks at under 5% opacity) is the entire environment — never composite a rendered scene behind the sketch.
**Palette:** line in graphite grey (`#2b2b2b` primary, `#555555`/`#777777` for lighter construction strokes) or a warm sepia (`#4a3728`), over a flat cream/light-grey paper (`#f2efe6` or `#ece7d8`). Choose the single colour accent freely per sketch from mid-saturation, mid-value hues (roughly 40-70% saturation, 50-75% lightness — e.g. `#c9782b` burnt orange, `#4a7a8c` slate teal, `#8a5ca6` dusty violet) rather than treating one hex as fixed; whichever hue is chosen, it is the only saturated colour in the sketch, confined to the `colour-accent` layer described above.
**Typography:** hand-lettered marginalia set in a script/marker face — `'Caveat', 'Kalam', 'Patrick Hand', 'Segoe Print', cursive` as the stack — weight 400-600, size 22-34px on a 1080-wide canvas, line-height 1.2-1.3, coloured to match the line ink (`#2b2b2b` or `#4a3728`) rather than the colour accent; never a separate typeset caption block.

**Choosing this over its neighbours:** against `studio-ghibli-anime.md` — that file is the finished-FILM stage — tight, varied-weight inked linework with hard-edged two-tone cel shading, dense gouache-painted backgrounds, muted naturalistic colour, atmospheric haze layered by depth, and NO visible construction lines. This register is the opposite pole, several pipeline stages earlier: linework is loose and searching rather than tightly inked; colour is a single vibrant accent rather than a muted, fully-rendered palette; texture is bare paper or graphite grain rather than painted gouache; the subject carries flat or no shading rather than consistent cel shading; the background is the paper itself rather than a dense painted setting; and the construction scaffolding stays visible as the whole point, rather than being cleaned away before the ink stage begins. The single fastest tell to check: if perspective guides/centre lines are visible under the ink, it is this register — Ghibli-anime never shows its scaffolding.
