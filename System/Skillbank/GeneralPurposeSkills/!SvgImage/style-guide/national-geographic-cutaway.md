# Classic National Geographic / scientific cutaway-exploded diagram
**Tags:** data-viz | technical
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** never let light direction, ghosting opacity, or system-colour hue drift inconsistently across one illustration, never route a leader line across the detail it labels, and never omit the human-scale figure or locator inset.
**Visual DNA:** A richly rendered, sectional cutaway (peel-back/exploded variants exist but are secondary) revealing interior structure through ghosted-transparency or torn/cut edges, with colour-coded systems and dense numbered annotation, shown from a dramatic angled 3/4 "hero" view.
**Typical subject / composition:** engineered or organic subjects worth immersive scientific spectacle — engines, ships, spacecraft, buildings, animal anatomy, geological strata, ruins. Three-quarter hero view: ~45° horizontal rotation with a 15-30° upward tilt, on a 1080-wide canvas. A human-scale silhouette sits near or within the subject, clear of critical detail. Callouts radiate spoke-like with minimum angular separation so they never crowd; foreground full saturation, mid-ground ghosted, background lower saturation; margins 8-15% of canvas width.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="dramatic-backdrop">` — soft environmental context, low opacity/blur, unlike a blank ground
- `<g id="outer-shell-ghosted">` — the subject's outer surface at 20-35% opacity, desaturated, so interior structure reads through it
- `<g id="cutaway-interior-layers">` — one sub-group per revealed system/organ/mechanism, each in its own hue family
- `<g id="peel-back-flap">` — an illustrated lifted surface section, if the reveal uses that device, with its own edge thickness and drop shadow
- `<g id="numbered-key-markers">` — small circled numerals/letters at each callout point
- `<g id="annotation-callouts">` — leader lines from marker to the dense key panel
- `<g id="legend-key-panel">`
- `<g id="title-caption">`
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Cutaway edge choice (the decision rule): ragged/torn section edge — irregular organic boundary — for organic subjects (strata, animal internals, ruins) where the narrative is "tearing through"; clean quarter-cut — a geometric 90° or 45° plane, bevelled to show material thickness — for engineered subjects (engines, ships, spacecraft, buildings); ghosting (outer shell at 20-35% opacity, desaturated) when the exterior context must stay visible alongside the interior. Mature compositions mix them: clean quarter-cut foreground, ghosted shell mid-ground, ragged section behind.
- Airbrush-derived shading: each component as a layered `linearGradient`/`radialGradient` with a 3-stop minimum — dark shadow at the underside, mid-tone, bright highlight on the top-facing surface — giving real volume without photoreal texture.
- Specular highlights: small near-white spots at roughly 45° from a top-left light source, opacity 0.6-0.8, never fully opaque.
- Ambient occlusion at joins: `feGaussianBlur` plus `feOffset`, soft, at 30-50% opacity.
- System colour-coding: one hue family per functional system, held consistent through the diagram and its legend — hydraulic `#E74C3C`, electrical `#3498DB`, structural `#95A5A6`, thermal `#F39C12`, pneumatic `#16A085`, mechanical motion `#9B59B6`, each with a lighter tint and darker shade; focus subject at 80-100% saturation, background systems under 50%.
- Annotation: callout lines 1-2 units weight on a 1080-wide canvas, fine arrowhead, in dark grey `#333333` (deliberately not the system colour, to keep hierarchy clear); straight, bent at 90° only to avoid crossing detail, curved only for organic subjects; direct labels for main systems, numbered callouts for dense sub-components; a locator/scale inset at 5-10% of illustration size, flatter and simpler, bottom-right or top-left.
**Palette:** NG signature yellow `#F4C430`; darkest neutral `#1A1A1A` or `#2C3E50` (never pure black); mid grey `#7F8C8D`; light grey `#ECF0F1`. Background by subject: white (technical), `#F0F8FF` (underwater), `#F5F5F5` (neutral), `#FFFEF0` (organic/historical). Metallics via gradient plus specular: brass `#D4A574`→`#B8860B`, silver `#C0C0C0`→`#E8E8E8`, copper `#B87333`→`#FF6B35`.
**Typography:** headline and body `Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` (headline semibold); labels `"Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`. Primary system name semibold in its system colour; sub-components regular dark grey; tertiary labels lighter grey, optionally small caps. Caption block: subject name semibold, description at 1.5 line-height, then the numbered list.

**Choosing this over its neighbours:** against Tufte (`tufte-object-designs.md`, `tufte-location-cutaway.md`) — NG runs a deliberately low data-ink ratio, using aesthetic layering, atmosphere, and system-coded colour to make you feel you are seeing the real object; Tufte strips to near-monochrome line art so you can analyse and compare. Against `dorling-kindersley-landscapes.md` — both model form with soft gradients, so the difference is not rendering but staging and intent: NG sets the subject in an environment, with atmospheric depth and system-coded colour, for immersive spectacle; DK cuts the subject out onto pure white with no environment at all and rings it with dense dot-and-leader captions, so you can find any part instantly. NG reveals context; DK catalogues; Tufte instruments.
