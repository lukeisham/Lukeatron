# Studio Ghibli style anime
**Tags:** illustrative | animation-film
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** never gradient or airbrush-shade a character (flat base + one hard-edged shadow only), never pure-black outlines or shadows, never equal contrast across every depth layer, never a saturated background competing with the characters, never symmetric/repetitive foliage or a fully centred focal point, never a polished conventionally-attractive face or heavily sculpted eyes.
**Visual DNA:** Gouache-and-watercolour-painted backgrounds with soft natural light and unequal depth contrast, under simple bold two-tone cel-shaded characters whose varied-weight ink linework and warm rim light carry the emotion.
**Typical subject / composition:** Original characters small within wide, static, establishing natural or rural environments — meadows, forests, skies, water, village rooftops; reserve close-ups for emotional beats. Horizon sits at roughly one third of the 1080-wide canvas height, placed high or low, never centred. Leave deliberate empty space (sky, water, drifting cloud) as content in its own right, and bias composition asymmetrically rather than centring the subject.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="sky-gradient">` — soft 4–6 stop gradient, warm/golden-hour bias, spanning the full 1080-wide canvas
- `<g id="background-far">` — background register: cool, desaturated, minimal detail, `feGaussianBlur` stdDeviation 8–14
- `<g id="midground">` — transitional register: foliage/architecture, `feGaussianBlur` stdDeviation 3–6
- `<g id="foreground-env">` — foreground register: warm, saturated, most detailed, stdDeviation 0 (sharp)
- `<g id="atmosphere-light">` — god rays, dust motes, glow glazes
- `<g id="characters">` — sub-groups in paint order: `character-base` (flat fill), `character-shadow-tone` (single hard-edged darker shape), `character-linework` (variable-weight ink outline, drawn last)
- `<g id="foreground-detail">` — nearest overlapping foliage/props, occasionally cropped by the frame edge
- `<g id="haze-glaze">` — final semi-transparent cool blue-grey/white wash unifying the whole scene
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Depth via unequal blur and contrast: apply `feGaussianBlur` at stdDeviation 8–14 (background), 3–6 (midground), 0 (foreground) — background contrast/saturation should read visibly flatter than foreground, not merely blurred, since unequal contrast across depth is what sells the depth.
- Two-tone cel shading: each character shape is a flat base fill plus exactly one hard-edged shadow shape (no gradient, no midtone) that shifts 15–30° cooler in hue and 30–50% darker in value at matched saturation versus the base — too small a hue shift reads as flat darkening rather than the Ghibli shadow.
- Variable-weight linework: build character outlines as a `<path>` stroked 1.5–4px on a 1080-wide canvas, tapering toward vertex/curve midpoints and thickening at direction changes — approximate a G-pen nib by varying `stroke-width` along the path (multiple overlapping path segments of different widths) rather than one uniform stroke.
- Dappled foliage texture: 15–30 small repeated leaf-cluster shapes per tree/hedge (not a flat pattern fill), each cluster's `fill-opacity` varied randomly within 0.75–1.0 so no two clusters read identically.
- God rays and haze glazes: soft elongated shapes 40–90px wide at the light source narrowing along their length, `fill-opacity` 0.08–0.2, layered 3–5 deep and painted into the background group (not a separate post-effect layer); finish with one full-canvas cool blue-grey or white rectangle at `fill-opacity` 0.05–0.12 over everything but the character linework, standing in for the final atmospheric glaze.
- Warm rim/backlight: a thin (6–14px on a 1080-wide canvas) warm-gold outer glow (`feGaussianBlur` stdDeviation 4–8 on a duplicated warm-fill silhouette) along the sun-facing edge of hair/silhouette, colour-matched to the sky gradient's warm stop so it reads as integrated light rather than an applied glow.
- SVG blend-mode limits: use `style="mix-blend-mode: screen"` on light-ray and glaze shapes for renderers that support CSS blend modes (browsers, most SVG viewers). Static rasterizers and some print pipelines ignore `mix-blend-mode` — for those targets, drop the blend mode and rely on the layered low-opacity shapes above (god rays, haze glaze) alone; do not depend on additive blending to carry the effect.
**Palette:** clear-day sky `#67B9E9`–`#8FB1E9`; dawn/dusk sky `#E48C2A`–`#F0D77B`; storm/shadow sky `#2D3740`–`#345C75`; pale sky `#DBEBF8`; cloud white `#DAEDF3`; pale gold `#F7EABD`; meadow green `#8CBF88`; spring grass `#6FB382`; deep forest `#26432F` / `#17382F`; skin (fair) `#E3BBA1`, `#E1D7CB`, `#ECD89D`; skin (medium) `#C3AF97`, `#D0C1AA`; earth/wood `#583B2B`, `#C3AF97`, `#D6C0A9`; character linework `#4A2E1E` (dark warm brown, never pure black); accents — blood red `#CD4F38`, gold sunset `#E48C2A`, deep magic blue `#403369`, sea `#278B9A`, sea foam `#94C5CC`. Keep every hue warm, muted and naturalistic — no neon, no high-chroma electric blues, no pure black anywhere in the image.
**Typography:** soft, slightly irregularly spaced, hand-drawn-feeling serif — stack `'Kalam', 'Segoe Script', 'Zapfino', 'Cochin', serif` — in a warm muted ink tone (use the linework brown `#4A2E1E` or a matching dark warm neutral), never high-contrast black on white; keep any caption small relative to the 1080-wide canvas so the illustration carries the piece.
