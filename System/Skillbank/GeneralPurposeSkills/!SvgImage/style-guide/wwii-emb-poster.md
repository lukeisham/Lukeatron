# WWII Allied / Empire Marketing Board poster style
**Tags:** poster | illustrated
**Anti-patterns / Avoid:** gradient/airbrushed shading inside a flat colour field, photo-realistic detail or heavy modelling, texture/cross-hatch/stipple/decorative ornament, seven or more colours, or a perfectly centred static composition with no directional vector.
**Visual DNA:** EMB/Shell/London Transport-school illustration (ca. 1926-1945) — a real illustrated scene built from large flat lithographic colour masses, hard edges, no atmospheric recession, with the slogan carried in a separate solid-colour band below or above the image rather than fused into it. The compositional FIELDS are flat throughout; only the central FIGURE may carry one or two flat shadow planes for volume — that shading never returns to the field structure around it, and the field structure itself never adopts Soviet-style diagonal fragmentation.
**Signature tells (must be visibly present):** 1) a solid-colour slogan band, full canvas width, kept structurally separate from the illustrated scene above/below it — never fused into the image the way Soviet type is 2) large flat lithographic colour fields throughout the scene, with shading confined to one or two flat shadow planes on the central figure only 3) depth built purely by overlap, relative scale, and vertical stacking — never colour-temperature or atmospheric recession, and (outside Newbould's one shallow horizon) never a gradient.
**Craft-rule carve-outs:** WWII/EMB poster uses hard-edged flat shapes throughout the field structure — edge-variation (varied line weight between adjacent shapes) is not applied there; the fields' flatness is the identifying trait. Contact shadows are omitted everywhere; planes are separated by overlap and occlusion, not by a dark join. Global light direction is moot for Games (silhouettes carry no shading at all) and is not modelled for Newbould's flat fields; the only shading in the whole poster lives on the central figure's own shadow plane(s), per Figure modelling below.
**Detail budget (per register):** Newbould prioritises atmospheric warmth and figure modelling over abundance of marks — 150-300 drawn elements on a 1080-wide canvas is typical and acceptable. Games uses bold silhouettes and negative space — 180-400 elements is typical. Neither register should be padded to meet the general 250-600 floor; composition and hierarchy override element count.
**Scale reference:** Format is generalisable beyond the Double Crown example below — on any canvas, keep margins at roughly 2-3% of canvas width and size the slogan band to the stated 18-28% of canvas height.
**Typical subject / composition:** landscapes, factories, farms, transport and travel scenes, home-front instructional subjects — built to persuade warmly (Newbould) or command clearly (Games), never to agitate.
**Standard layers (z-order, bottom → top):**
- `<g id="background-scene">` — a simplified illustrative scene occupying roughly 60-70% of canvas height
- `<g id="flat-colour-blocks">` — large flat fields structuring the scene via overlap and vertical stacking, no gradients. Each major field is its own semantically-named sub-group so it can be recoloured or adjusted independently, e.g. `<g id="flat-colour-blocks"><g id="field-sky">…</g><g id="field-ground">…</g><g id="field-building">…</g></g>`.
- `<g id="central-figure-or-motif">` — the main illustrated subject; flat base colour plus at most two flat shadow planes for volume. May nest sub-groups (`head`, `torso`, `limbs`) for a modelled figure, e.g. `<g id="central-figure-or-motif"><g id="head">…</g><g id="torso">…</g><g id="limbs">…</g></g>`, or be a single unified group for a simplified/silhouetted (Games-register) figure.
- `<g id="slogan-text-band">` — a solid-colour panel, full width, in the lower 18-28% of canvas height (or upper, by brief), carrying 1-2 display lines
- `<g id="supporting-text">` — smaller secondary/sponsor line within or beneath the band
- `<g id="national-emblem">` — a generic crest/roundel/flag-motif device, optional, never a specific real insignia
**Distinctive SVG techniques:**
- Two named sub-registers, mutually exclusive within one poster: **Newbould** (romantic pastoral — warm ochres/siennas, pastoral figures in the middle ground, one deliberate soft-sky exception to the flat rule, slightly modelled figures, hand-lettered display, nostalgic tone) or **Games** ("maximum meaning, minimum means" — one or two figures reduced to silhouette or a single plane, hard geometry, acute angles, high-contrast negative space, a single brilliant isolated colour, bold geometric lettering, urgent tone). Choose one register per poster and do not blend their palettes or figure treatments.
- Depth without gradients, in this priority order: overlap first, then relative scale, then vertical stacking on the picture plane, then (Newbould only) a shallow horizon line — never colour-temperature or atmospheric recession. Newbould's horizon sits at 35-50% of canvas height (roughly middle ground) and is the sole exception to the flat-field rule: a soft gradient in ochre/cream/pale-gold tones suggests atmospheric depth there and nowhere else in the poster.
- Slogan band as load-bearing structure: a solid rectangle spanning the full 1080px canvas width, sized to 18-28% of canvas height, reversed-out type (black on cream, or white on blue/red), with a 1-2pt rule directly above it separating it from the image.
- Central-figure shading resolved: base flat colour plane plus exactly one or two additional flat shadow-plane `<path>`s (never a gradient) — this is the one place in the style where more than a single flat tone appears, and it is confined to the figure, not the surrounding fields. The shadow plane covers the form's underside/shaded side (e.g. the right side of a face if the implied light is from the left) and shifts 30-40% darker in value while holding saturation; a single plane may cover one limb or the torso, two planes might separate torso from limbs. The edge between base and shadow is always hard — never blended or gradiented.
- Hand-lettered display (Newbould only): drawn with a uniform 2.5-3.5px stroke (1080-wide canvas), slight per-glyph rotation (±1-2°) and baseline offset (±1-2px) to preserve hand-drawn warmth. No typeset font is used for Newbould display text; Games register uses the bold geometric typeset lettering below instead.
- Colour-plate discipline: 4-6 flat spot colours per poster in the Newbould register, 2-3 in the Games register, each treated as a separate flat fill with no shared gradients between plates.
**Palette:** cream `#FAF5ED` or `#F5EFE7`; railway blue `#003E73` or `#004B87`; poster red `#D84040` or `#E03D38`; ochre `#D4A76A` or `#C9945A`; forest green `#2E5A3F` or `#3A6B52`; burgundy `#5C1C1C`; mustard `#E8B923`; near-black `#2B2B2B`. Pick 4-6 (Newbould) or 2-3 (Games) from this set per poster — never mix Newbould earth tones with Games' single brilliant isolated colour in the same piece.
**Typography:** Gill Sans Bold/Extra Bold as the workhorse (never light or regular weight in display); Johnston for transport-authority work; hand-lettered display on Newbould-register pieces (see Hand-lettered display above for stroke/wobble specifics). Headline caps tracked +0.10 to +0.25em; Games-register all-caps tracked looser, +0.30em. Stack: `"Gill Sans Nova", "Gill Sans", "Trebuchet MS", sans-serif`. Example format: Double Crown 20 x 30in, 2:3 portrait — 1080 x 1620 on the standard canvas, margins approx. 27px (≈2-3% of canvas width — see Scale reference above for scaling to other canvases).
**Choosing this over its neighbours:** against `soviet-propaganda-poster.md` — Soviet fuses bold rotated type directly into one 30-60° diagonal thrust with no separate slogan band and monumental cropped figures; this style keeps every field flat and static, isolates the slogan in its own solid-colour band, and confines all shading to the central figure alone. Against `british-official-announcement.md` — that style is pure typographic notice, centred and illustration-free; this style always builds a real illustrated scene (landscape, factory, figure) with the slogan band as a structural adjunct, never the whole composition. Reach for this style for warm (Newbould) or clear-command (Games) illustrated persuasion; reach for Soviet when the brief wants diagonal agitation energy, and British-official when no imagery should appear at all.
