## Eleven major organ systems

| Organ System | Primary Components | Key Functions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skeletal** | Bones, cartilage, ligaments, joints | Provides structural framework, protects organs, produces blood cells, stores minerals. |
| **Muscular** | Skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscles | Enables voluntary and involuntary movement, generates body heat. |
| **Integumentary**| Skin, hair, nails | Acts as first line of defense against pathogens, regulates temperature, prevents dehydration. |
| **Nervous** | Brain, spinal cord, nerves | Rapid control center; processes sensory input and commands actions, thoughts, and reflexes. |
| **Endocrine** | Glands (pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, etc.)| Slow-acting control center; secretes hormones to regulate metabolism, growth, and reproduction. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Heart, arteries, veins, blood | Transports oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to cells; removes carbon dioxide and waste. |
| **Respiratory** | Lungs, airways (trachea, bronchi) | Facilitates gas exchange; absorbs vital oxygen into the blood and exhales toxic carbon dioxide. |
| **Digestive** | Mouth, stomach, intestines, liver | Breaks down food into absorbable macronutrients and micronutrients for cellular fuel. |
| **Immune/Lymphatic**| White blood cells, lymph nodes/vessels | Defends the body against dangerous pathogens; manages fluid levels and transports immune cells. |
| **Urinary** | Kidneys, bladder, ureters, urethra | Filters waste, excess water, and salts from the blood to expel as urine; maintains chemical balance. |
| **Reproductive** | Male and female reproductive organs | Produces sex cells (sperm/eggs) and hormones required for the continuation of the species. |

## Core Principles of Physiology

| Core Principle | Key Mechanism | Condensed Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Feedback Loops** | Control & Regulation | The body's self-correcting mechanisms used to maintain balance (mostly negative/stabilizing loops). |
| **Metabolism** | Energy Management | The continuous chemical cycle of breaking matter down for fuel and building it up for repair. |
| **Biological Gradients** | Physical Flow | The natural movement of substances from areas of high pressure/concentration to low areas. |
| **Cellular Communication** | Internal Signaling | The network of chemical messengers (hormones, neurotransmitters) that allow cells to coordinate. |
| **Interdependence** | System Integration | The absolute reliance of every organ system on the others; a shift in one impacts them all. |

*Homeostasis* = the 11 systems and the core principles of physiology functioning together to maintain balance and health.

## Systems Biology or Integrative Human Biology.

| Component | Core Focus | Simplified Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Genetics & Epigenetics** | The Blueprint | Your DNA instructions, plus how your habits and environment turn those genes "on" or "off." |
| **Cellular Biology** | Micro-Factories | The microscopic cells and internal structures (organelles) that actually do the physical work of keeping you alive. |
| **The Microbiome** | Symbiotic Ecosystem | The trillions of non-human bacteria, fungi, and viruses living in and on you that help digest food and regulate immunity. |
| **Chronobiology** | Biological Clock | Your circadian rhythms; how your body syncs its hormones and functions to the 24-hour cycle of day and night. |
| **The Exposome** | Environmental Interface | The total sum of everything you are exposed to (diet, stress, toxins, pollution) over your lifetime and how it affects your body. |

## Clinical reasoning `SOAP`

| SOAP Step | Clinical Focus | Simplified Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subjective** | Patient History | What the patient reports feeling (chief complaint, symptom history, past medical issues). |
| **Objective** | Physical Data | What the doctor can measure and observe (vital signs, physical exam findings). |
| **Assessment** | The Diagnosis | Synthesizing the subjective and objective data to form a differential or definitive diagnosis. |
| **Plan** | Treatment & Next Steps | The strategy to solve the problem (ordering tests, prescribing medications, follow-up). |

# Open Source Options

The most prominent open-source CDSS tools and related ecosystems:

1. **OpenCDS** — One of the most widely recognized, dedicated open-source CDSS frameworks. A collaborative, multi-institutional project designed to provide clinical decision support at scale and used by thousands of healthcare facilities (including the Veterans Affairs).
   - **Core Function:** Ingests proprietary source data from an Electronic Health Record (EHR), transforms it into a standard format, evaluates it against medical rules, and returns treatment suggestions.
   - **Interoperability:** Built heavily on healthcare standards, supporting HL7, FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), and CDSHooks.
   - **Rules Engine:** Uses a Java-based architecture and allows clinical rules to be written in languages like Java or Drools.

2. **OpenEMR** — While primarily an Electronic Health Record (EHR) system, OpenEMR includes robust, natively integrated clinical decision support capabilities.
   - **Core Function:** Provides clinicians with automated clinical reminders, drug-drug interaction checks, and preventive care alerts directly within the patient charting workflow.
   - **Community:** One of the most popular open-source health platforms globally, supported by a massive, active community of developers and medical professionals.

3. **GNU Health** — A comprehensive, free Health and Hospital Information System supported by the GNU Solidarity project.
   - **Core Function:** Goes beyond simple record-keeping to include built-in CDS features, such as disease management protocols, automated alerts for abnormal lab values or vital signs, and genetic risk evaluations.
   - **Deployment:** Heavily utilized in developing nations and by organizations looking for a free, highly customizable hospital management and clinical reasoning solution.

4. **Drools (The Inference Engine)** — While not a pre-packaged CDSS on its own, Drools is a widely used open-source Business Rules Management System (BRMS) that powers the logic behind many healthcare CDSS platforms (including OpenCDS).
   - **Core Function:** Acts as the "brain" or event-processing engine, allowing developers to write complex clinical logic (e.g., IF patient is > 65 AND kidney function is < X AND prescribed Drug Y, THEN trigger Alert Z) that runs in real-time against patient data.

## Hospital and Clinical Frameworks on GitHub

- **OpenEMR** (`openemr/openemr`) — One of the most active healthcare repositories on GitHub. You can view the entire codebase, including how they structure their clinical decision rules, patient reminders, and drug interaction APIs using PHP and JavaScript.
- **CDS Hooks** (`cds-hooks`) — While OpenCDS as a legacy software has fragmented, the modern standard it helped inspire — CDS Hooks — is heavily developed on GitHub. This repository contains the exact specifications for how modern EHRs should trigger external decision support algorithms via API calls.
- **Drools** (`apache/incubator-kie-drools`) — The business rules engine that powers many healthcare systems is actively maintained here (formerly under kiegroup). You can explore the Java-based rules engine that processes massive amounts of logical clinical statements in real-time.
- **GNU Health** — As an official GNU project, its primary code repository is hosted on GNU Savannah (the Free Software Foundation's forge), rather than GitHub. However, you will often find mirrors and related integration modules built by the community on GitHub.

### Patient-Led and Personal CDSS

- **OpenAPS** (`openaps/openaps`) — The entire Artificial Pancreas System is open-sourced on GitHub. It is a brilliant example of patient-led engineering, primarily written in Python and JavaScript, showing exactly how to read continuous glucose data and execute physical pump commands.
- **Loop** (`LoopKit/Loop`) — Another massive patient-led project similar to OpenAPS, but specifically built as an iOS app (written in Swift) that serves as an automated insulin delivery system and decision support tool for Apple devices.
- **Nightscout** (`nightscout/cgm-remote-monitor`) — Often used in tandem with OpenAPS and Loop, this is a web-based, open-source dashboard that acts as a real-time remote monitor (a visual CDSS dashboard) for continuous glucose data.

### How Developers Use These Repositories

If you are looking to build something yourself, developers rarely fork an entire hospital system like OpenEMR. Instead, they usually leverage the CDS Hooks specifications to build a standalone, containerized microservice. This allows a developer to write a customized diagnostic algorithm in Python or Node.js, and use the standard GitHub CDS Hooks templates to make their algorithm securely "talk" to any major commercial EHR (like Epic or Cerner).
