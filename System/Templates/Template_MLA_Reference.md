# MLA Reference Guide

---

## Inline Citations

Inline citations appear in parentheses within the text, directly after the quoted or paraphrased material.

### General Format

> (Author Last, Name Page Number)

If no page number, year of publication, if no page number and no year, omit the page number and year entirely.

---

### Website

> (Author Last Name, Year of Publication)

If no author, use a shortened version of the page title in title case, in quotation marks:

> ("Page Title")

If no year of publication, omit the year entirely.

**Examples:**

> The report notes a significant increase in applications (Smith).

> Studies suggest the trend is accelerating ("Climate Data Overview").

---

### Journal Article

> (Author Last Name Page Number)

**Examples:**

> The argument has been widely contested (Jones, 47).

> Multiple scholars note the same pattern (Brown and Lee, 112).

For three or more authors, use the first author's name followed by "et al.":

> (Williams et al., 89)

---

### Book

> (Author Last, Name Page Number)

**Examples:**

> This mirrors the findings from earlier fieldwork (Thompson, 204).

> The tension is described as irresolvable (Martinez and Okafor, 37).

---

## References

All sources cited in the text appear in a Works Cited list at the end of the document. Entries are alphabetised by the author's last name (or by title if no author). Double-space entries; use a hanging indent for lines after the first.

---

### Website

**Format:**

> Last, First. "Page Title." *Site Name*, Publisher or Sponsor (if different from site name), Day Month Year, URL.

**Notes:**
- Omit fields you cannot find.
- Abbreviate months: Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., May, June, July, Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.
- If no author, begin with the page title.
- Include the URL without "https://".

**Examples:**

> Smith, Jane. "The Future of Renewable Energy." *Energy Today*, International Energy Agency, 12 Mar. 2024, www.energytoday.org/future-renewable.

> "Climate Data Overview." *NASA Global Climate Change*, NASA, 5 Jan. 2025, climate.nasa.gov/climate-resources.

---

### Journal Article

**Format:**

> Last, First, and First Last. "Article Title." *Journal Name*, vol. #, no. #, Month Year, pp. #–#. *Database Name* (if accessed online), doi:xxxxxxxxxx or URL.

**Notes:**
- List all authors up to two; for three or more, list only the first followed by "et al."
- Include DOI when available; otherwise include the stable URL.

**Examples:**

> Jones, Michael. "Contested Ground: The Rhetoric of Urban Planning." *Journal of Urban Studies*, vol. 58, no. 3, Sept. 2022, pp. 44–61. *JSTOR*, www.jstor.org/stable/12345678.

> Brown, Sarah, and David Lee. "Patterns in Longitudinal Survey Data." *Social Research Quarterly*, vol. 14, no. 1, Mar. 2023, pp. 108–125. doi:10.1000/xyz123.

> Williams, Paul, et al. "Neural Correlates of Decision-Making." *Cognitive Neuroscience*, vol. 9, no. 4, Dec. 2021, pp. 80–99. *PubMed*, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/99887766.

---

### Book

**Format:**

> Last, First, and First Last. *Title of Book: Subtitle if Any*. Publisher, Year.

**Notes:**
- Capitalise all major words in the title.
- For two authors, list both; for three or more, list the first followed by "et al."
- For an edited book, add "edited by First Last" after the title.
- For a chapter in an edited book, see the variation below.

**Examples:**

> Thompson, Claire. *Fieldwork and Its Discontents: Methods in Cultural Anthropology*. Oxford University Press, 2019.

> Martinez, Elena, and Kwame Okafor. *The Grammar of Conflict: Language and Power*. Routledge, 2020.

> Williams, Paul, et al. *Foundations of Cognitive Science*. MIT Press, 2018.

**Chapter in an Edited Book:**

> Last, First. "Chapter Title." *Book Title*, edited by First Last, Publisher, Year, pp. #–#.

> Hughes, Fiona. "Memory and Narrative." *Perspectives on the Self*, edited by Laura Marsh, Cambridge University Press, 2021, pp. 55–78.
