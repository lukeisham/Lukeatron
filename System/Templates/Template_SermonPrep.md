HOW TO USE
0. REQUIRED — before any research, read Memory/Long-Term/Preaching/Sources.md and draw
   lexical, commentary, journal, and historical material from its listed sources in
   priority order. Never cite its blacklist. Append genuinely good new sources found
   during the work (Long-Term write → !Checkpoint).
1. Fill the header block: series big idea (if a series outline exists), passage big
   idea, and a representative verse.
2. Set the passage big idea FIRST. Then divide the passage into sub-ideas that each
   support the big idea, and group the verses under those sub-ideas.
3. If the big idea later changes, reconfigure the sub-ideas and the verse grouping to
   match it.
4. Annotate verse-by-verse using blockquotes (see the annotation key below).
5. Complete both theology tables and the References list.

ANNOTATION KEY (the style this template prescribes)
- Each note sits in a blockquote ( > ) beneath the verse it explains.
- Lead each note with a bold label naming the kind of note. Labels are free-form —
  choose what fits the verse. Common ones: **Greek**, **Cross-references**,
  **Historical note**, **Metaphor**, **Trope**, **Rhetorical structure**, **Theme**.
- Greek notes use the form:  word = Greek (English pronunciation) — gloss/discussion.
  Flag a word when it is unusual, semantically pivotal, disputed, or alters the
  grammar/structure of the sentence.
- Historical / source notes carry an inline MLA citation — (Author Last Name, Year) for
  books and journal articles, (Author Last Name) or ("Page Title") for websites —
  collected in full under References at the end. Follow Template_MLA_Reference.md for
  both inline citation and References entry formats.
