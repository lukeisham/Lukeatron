## GAMSAT S3 Passage — Osmotic Pressure in Plant Cells

**Difficulty:** Hard

### Stimulus

A researcher investigated the effect of external solute concentration on the mass of potato tuber tissue. Cylinders of potato tissue (initial mass: 2.00 ± 0.05 g) were immersed in sucrose solutions of varying concentrations. After 60 minutes, the cylinders were removed, blotted dry, and reweighed. The experiment was conducted at 22°C. Each condition was replicated 5 times.

**TABLE 1: Change in Mass of Potato Cylinders After Immersion in Sucrose Solutions**

| Sucrose Concentration (mol/L) | Final Mass — Mean (g) | Change in Mass (%) | Standard Deviation (g) |
|-------------------------------|----------------------|--------------------|------------------------|
| 0.0 (distilled water) | 2.34 | +17.0 | ±0.08 |
| 0.1 | 2.22 | +11.0 | ±0.06 |
| 0.2 | 2.12 | +6.0 | ±0.05 |
| 0.3 | 2.01 | +0.5 | ±0.04 |
| 0.4 | 1.89 | −5.5 | ±0.05 |
| 0.5 | 1.76 | −12.0 | ±0.06 |
| 0.6 | 1.65 | −17.5 | ±0.07 |
| 0.8 | 1.58 | −21.0 | ±0.09 |

**Additional information:** The water potential (Ψ) of a solution is given by Ψ = −iCRT, where i is the ionisation constant (i = 1 for sucrose), C is the molar concentration, R is the pressure constant (0.00831 L·MPa/mol·K), and T is the absolute temperature in Kelvin. Pure water at standard pressure has Ψ = 0 MPa.

---

### Questions

**1.** Based on the data in Table 1, what is the approximate sucrose concentration inside the potato tuber cells (i.e., the concentration at which there is no net movement of water)?

- **A:** 0.0 mol/L
- **B:** 0.2 mol/L
- **C:** 0.3 mol/L
- **D:** 0.4 mol/L

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:** The isotonic point occurs when there is no net change in mass — i.e., the external and internal solute concentrations are equal. At 0.3 mol/L, the mass change is +0.5%, which is closest to zero (within measurement error, since SD is ±0.04 g on a 2.00 g initial mass, or ±2%, making +0.5% not significantly different from zero). At 0.2 mol/L there is a clear gain (+6.0%) and at 0.4 mol/L a clear loss (−5.5%). 0.3 mol/L is the isotonic point.

---

**2.** At 0.4 mol/L sucrose, water moved out of the potato cells. Which of the following best explains why?

- **A:** The sucrose solution had a higher water potential than the cell contents.
- **B:** The cell membrane became impermeable to water at this concentration.
- **C:** The water potential of the external solution was lower (more negative) than the water potential inside the cells.
- **D:** Active transport of sucrose into the cells caused water to follow by osmosis.

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:** Water moves from regions of higher water potential to regions of lower water potential. At 0.4 mol/L, the external solution has a more negative Ψ than the cell interior (isotonic at ~0.3 mol/L), so water flows out. C correctly captures this. A reverses the gradient. B is incorrect — water movement demonstrates permeability. D describes the wrong direction and mechanism.

---

**3.** Using the formula Ψ = −iCRT and given T = 22°C = 295 K, what is the approximate water potential of the 0.5 mol/L sucrose solution?

- **A:** −0.61 MPa
- **B:** −1.23 MPa
- **C:** −2.46 MPa
- **D:** −4.92 MPa

**Correct Answer:** **B**

**Explanation:** Ψ = −(1)(0.5)(0.00831)(295) = −0.5 × 0.00831 × 295 = −0.5 × 2.45145 = −1.226 MPa ≈ −1.23 MPa.

---

**4.** A student proposes that increasing the temperature to 37°C would increase the rate of water movement but not change the final equilibrium mass at each concentration. Is this proposal scientifically valid?

- **A:** Yes — temperature affects only the kinetic energy of water molecules (and thus the rate of diffusion), not the thermodynamic equilibrium determined by solute concentrations.
- **B:** Yes — temperature increases the rate of all biological processes but osmotic equilibrium is determined solely by concentration gradients.
- **C:** No — increasing the temperature changes the water potential of the external solution (Ψ depends on T), shifting the equilibrium point.
- **D:** No — at 37°C, the cell membranes would denature and lose selective permeability.

**Correct Answer:** **C**

**Explanation:** The water potential formula Ψ = −iCRT explicitly depends on T. Increasing temperature makes Ψ more negative for the external solution, shifting the isotonic point. While A and B correctly note that temperature affects rates, they miss that Ψ itself is temperature-dependent. The equilibrium concentration would shift. D is speculative and extreme (membrane denaturation does not occur at 37°C).
