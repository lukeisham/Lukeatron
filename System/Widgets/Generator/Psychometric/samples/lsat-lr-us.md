# LSAT — Logical Reasoning — US

**Reference test:** LSAT — Logical Reasoning section (US law school admissions; note: Analytical Reasoning / Logic Games removed August 2024)
**Format:** Two scored LR sections, ~24–26 questions each, 35 minutes per section. Each question presents a short argumentative passage (typically 50–120 words) followed by a single multiple-choice question.
**Answer style:** Single correct answer (A–E). Binary scoring.
**Question types:** Weaken, strengthen, necessary assumption, sufficient assumption, flaw, method of reasoning, main conclusion, parallel reasoning, inference, point at issue, principle, paradox, role of statement, evaluate.

## LSAT LR — Necessary Assumption Question

**Difficulty:** Hard

### Stimulus

Political scientist: "Countries with proportional representation electoral systems tend to have higher voter turnout than countries with first-past-the-post systems. This is likely because, under proportional representation, voters who support smaller parties believe their vote will actually contribute to representation, whereas in first-past-the-post systems, supporters of smaller parties may abstain because they see their preferred candidate as having no realistic chance of winning. Therefore, if the United Kingdom adopted proportional representation, voter turnout in UK general elections would increase."

### Question

Which of the following is an assumption on which the argument depends?

### Options

- **A:** The United Kingdom's current electoral system is the only factor suppressing voter turnout.
- **B:** The relationship between electoral system type and voter turnout observed in other countries would also hold in the United Kingdom.
- **C:** Voters in proportional representation systems are more politically knowledgeable than voters in first-past-the-post systems.
- **D:** Smaller parties currently have no representation in the UK Parliament.
- **E:** Increasing voter turnout would be beneficial for democratic governance in the United Kingdom.

### Correct Answer

**B**

### Clue

The argument moves from a general correlation observed in other countries to a specific prediction about the UK. What must be true for this inference to be valid?

### Explainer

**B is the necessary assumption.** The argument relies on the premise that proportional representation systems correlate with higher turnout *in the countries studied*, and then concludes that adopting such a system *would cause* higher turnout *in the UK*. This inference requires the assumption that the UK is not an exception — that the same causal mechanism would operate there. The Negation Test confirms this: if the relationship observed in other countries would *not* hold in the UK (perhaps due to unique features of UK political culture, media environment, or institutional design), the conclusion collapses. B is therefore a necessary assumption.

- **A** goes too far: the argument doesn't need the electoral system to be the *only* factor — just that it is *a* factor that would have an effect.
- **C** is irrelevant to the causal mechanism described (which is about perceived vote efficacy, not knowledge).
- **D** is not required: the argument is about supporters of smaller parties *perceiving* their vote won't matter, not about whether they actually have representation.
- **E** is about the desirability of the outcome, not whether the outcome would occur. The argument is a predictive claim, not a normative one.
