## UCAT ANZ DM — Probability Reasoning

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Format:** Single best answer (A–E)

### Stimulus

A general practice clinic in regional Victoria serves a population of 2,400 patients. The clinic's records show the following:

- 65% of patients have had a blood pressure check in the last 12 months.
- Of those who had a blood pressure check, 20% were found to have hypertension (high blood pressure).
- Of those who did NOT have a blood pressure check, an estimated 12% have undiagnosed hypertension based on population health data.

### Question

Approximately how many of the clinic's 2,400 patients are estimated to have hypertension?

### Options

- **A:** 288
- **B:** 312
- **C:** 413
- **D:** 480
- **E:** 576

### Correct Answer

**C**

### Step-by-Step Calculation

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|------|-------------|--------|
| **1. Patients with BP check** | 2,400 × 65% | **1,560 patients** |
| **2. Diagnosed hypertension (from checked)** | 1,560 × 20% | **312 patients** |
| **3. Patients without BP check** | 2,400 × 35% | **840 patients** |
| **4. Estimated undiagnosed hypertension (from unchecked)** | 840 × 12% | **≈101 patients** |
| **5. Total estimated hypertension** | 312 + 101 | **≈413 patients** |

### Clue

This is a weighted-probability problem. You need to calculate the hypertension rate in two separate populations (those checked and those not checked) and combine them. Don't just multiply 2,400 by one percentage.

### Explainer

**C (413) is correct.** The total is composed of two groups: diagnosed cases among those screened (1,560 × 20% = 312) and estimated undiagnosed cases among those not screened (840 × 12% = 100.8, rounded to 101). 312 + 101 = 413.

Common errors:
- **A (288):** Multiplying 2,400 × 12% — applying the undiagnosed rate to the whole population.
- **B (312):** Only counting the diagnosed cases and ignoring the estimated undiagnosed group.
- **D (480):** Multiplying 2,400 × 20% — applying the diagnosed rate to the whole population.
- **E (576):** Multiplying 2,400 × (20% + 12%) / 2 × 2 — a confused averaging.

UCAT ANZ DM probability questions test the ability to analyse data across subpopulations and avoid naive averaging. An on-screen calculator is provided, and scratch paper/whiteboard is available for working.
