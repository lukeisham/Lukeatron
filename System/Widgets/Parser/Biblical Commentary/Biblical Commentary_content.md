## 1. John Calvin (CalvinCommentaries)

**Source:** `CalvinCommentaries/` (this folder) — SWORD module, OSIS XML, Public Domain (CCEL).

**Licence:** Public Domain. Converted to SWORD format by Luke Plant from material at Christian Classics Ethereal Library (ccel.org).

### 1.1 Approach and Emphasis

Calvin reads Ephesians 1:3–14 as a sustained doxological unfolding of election and adoption. His characteristic emphasis falls on:

- **Sovereign election (v. 4–5):** Calvin stresses that God's choice precedes any human merit — "he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world" is the foundation of the entire passage. Election is in Christ, not apart from him; the decree is eternal, unconditional, and directed toward holiness as its end ("that we should be holy and without blame before him in love").
- **Adoption as the goal of predestination (v. 5):** Calvin notes that predestination is "unto the adoption of children" — the purpose is relational, not merely forensic. The "good pleasure of his will" underscores that the motive lies entirely within God.
- **Redemption through his blood (v. 7):** The material cause of our acceptance is Christ's blood; Calvin insists on substitutionary atonement as the ground of forgiveness.
- **The mystery of his will (v. 9–10):** Calvin interprets the "mystery" as God's eternal purpose to sum up all things in Christ — the recapitulation (ἀνακεφαλαιώσασθαι) of a fractured creation under one head.
- **The sealing of the Spirit (v. 13–14):** The Holy Spirit is the "earnest" (ἀρραβών) — a down-payment guaranteeing the full inheritance. Calvin emphasises that this sealing follows faith ("after that ye believed") and confirms the believer's security.

### 1.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Treats the passage as one continuous sentence in Greek (vv. 3–14), mirroring Paul's trinitarian structure: the Father elects (vv. 3–6), the Son redeems (vv. 7–12), the Spirit seals (vv. 13–14).
- "To the praise of the glory of his grace" (v. 6, 12, 14) is read as the doxological refrain structuring the passage into three movements.
- Election "in him" (ἐν αὐτῷ, v. 4) is the controlling prepositional phrase — all blessings are mediated through union with Christ.

## 2. Adam Clarke (Clarke)

**Source:** `Clarke/` (this folder) — SWORD module. Licence to be verified.

**Licence:** `[UNVERIFIED — confirm licence status of Clarke's Commentary module]`

### 2.1 Approach and Emphasis

Clarke brings his characteristic Wesleyan-Arminian lens and philological attention to the Greek text:

- **"Blessed be the God and Father..." (v. 3):** Clarke notes the Jewish berakhah form — Paul adopts the synagogue blessing pattern and fills it with Christian content. "Spiritual blessings in heavenly places" are contrasted with the temporal, earthly blessings of the Mosaic covenant.
- **"Chosen...before the foundation of the world" (v. 4):** Clarke reads election as corporate — God chose a people (the Church) in Christ, and individuals participate by faith. He resists the Calvinist reading of unconditional individual election, arguing that the purpose ("that we should be holy") makes holiness the condition contemplated in the decree, not merely its result.
- **"Predestinated...unto the adoption" (v. 5):** Predestination is to privilege (adoption), not to eternal destiny in itself. Clarke distinguishes this from fatalism — the destination is settled; who travels there is not.
- **"The redemption through his blood" (v. 7):** Clarke emphasises ἀπολύτρωσις as release by payment of a ransom. He connects this to the Year of Jubilee typology.
- **"The earnest of our inheritance" (v. 14):** Clarke explores ἀρραβών as a commercial term — a first instalment that is part of the whole and guarantees the remainder.

### 2.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Extensive lexical notes on Greek terms (εὐλογία, ἐκλέγομαι, προορίζω, ἀπολύτρωσις, ἀρραβών)
- Consistently reads predestination passages through a corporate-and-conditional lens rather than individual-and-unconditional
- Connects OT types (Jubilee, covenant blessings) to NT fulfilment throughout

## 3. D TN (Through the Bible / Daily Bible Study Notes)

**Source:** `DTN/` (this folder) — SWORD module. Licence to be verified.

**Licence:** `[UNVERIFIED — confirm licence status of DTN module]`

### 3.1 Approach and Emphasis

`[UNVERIFIED — the identity of the "DTN" module needs confirmation. If this is the "Daily Study Bible" (William Barclay) or "Through the Bible" (J. Vernon McGee) or another work, the following characterisation may be incorrect. Check the module's .conf file for the actual source.]`

Assuming this is a devotional/accessible commentary:

- **"All spiritual blessings" (v. 3):** Paul begins not with human need but with divine blessing — the passage is a single outburst of praise. The blessings are "spiritual" because they come from the Holy Spirit and belong to the new age.
- **"Chosen in him" (v. 4):** The emphasis falls on union with Christ — election is never abstract but always "in him." The purpose is ethical transformation: "holy and without blame before him in love."
- **"The mystery of his will" (v. 9):** In Paul's usage, "mystery" (μυστήριον) is not something obscure but something once hidden and now revealed — specifically, God's plan to unite all things, Jew and Gentile alike, in Christ.
- **"In whom also we have obtained an inheritance" (v. 11):** The "we" likely refers to Jewish believers (the first to hope in Christ), with "ye also" in v. 13 referring to Gentile believers — the unity of Jew and Gentile in one inheritance is central to Ephesians.
- **"Sealed with that holy Spirit of promise" (v. 13):** The seal marks ownership and authenticity. The Spirit is both the seal and the content of the promise — God's presence now guarantees the future.

### 3.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Reads the passage as doxology before theology — Paul is worshipping, not systematising
- Emphasises the Jew-Gentile unity theme that dominates Ephesians
- Treats "adoption," "redemption," and "inheritance" as overlapping metaphors for the same reality viewed from different angles

## 4. KingComments (King's Comments)

**Source:** `KingComments/` (this folder) — SWORD module. Licence to be verified.

**Licence:** `[UNVERIFIED — confirm licence status and identity of KingComments module]`

### 4.1 Approach and Emphasis

`[UNVERIFIED — the identity of the "KingComments" module needs confirmation. This may be a commentary by a particular author surnamed King, or a collection. Check the .conf file.]`

Assuming a conservative/evangelical commentary:

- **Trinitarian structure:** The passage traces the work of each Person: the Father plans (vv. 3–6), the Son purchases (vv. 7–12), the Spirit preserves (vv. 13–14). The threefold refrain "to the praise of his glory" marks these divisions.
- **"Blessed be God" (v. 3):** εὐλογητός is used in the NT only of God — creatures are εὐλογημένοι (blessed by God); God alone is εὐλογητός (the blessed one, worthy of blessing).
- **"Before the foundation of the world" (v. 4):** Election is eternal — it originates in God's timeless decree, not in human response or foreseen faith.
- **"According to the riches of his grace" (v. 7):** Redemption is lavish, not measured — "riches" (πλοῦτος) suggests abundance beyond necessity.
- **"That in the dispensation of the fulness of times" (v. 10):** The KJV "dispensation" (οἰκονομία) is God's household management of history — all epochs converge on Christ as their organising centre.
- **"The earnest of our inheritance" (v. 14):** The Spirit is God's down-payment — not a promise of future payment but the first instalment of the payment itself, guaranteeing the rest.

### 4.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Strong trinitarian reading
- Insists on eternal, unconditional election (consistent with Reformed interpretation)
- Reads "we" (v. 11–12) as Jewish believers contrasted with "ye" (v. 13) Gentile believers, highlighting the mystery of Gentile inclusion

## 5. RWP (Robertson's Word Pictures)

**Source:** `RWP/` (this folder) — SWORD module. Licence to be verified.

**Licence:** `[UNVERIFIED — confirm licence status. A.T. Robertson's Word Pictures in the New Testament was published 1930–1933; copyright status depends on renewal.]`

### 5.1 Approach and Emphasis

Robertson's Word Pictures provides grammatical and lexical commentary on the Greek text. His notes on Ephesians 1:3–14 would include:

- **Verse 3:** εὐλογητός ("blessed") — a verbal adjective used only of God in the NT. ἐν πάσῃ εὐλογίᾳ πνευματικῇ ("with all spiritual blessings") — note the locative ἐν, "in the sphere of."
- **Verse 4:** ἐξελέξατο ("he chose") — aorist middle, God chose for himself. πρὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου ("before the foundation of the world") — the phrase appears also in John 17:24 and 1 Peter 1:20.
- **Verses 3–14:** Robertson famously noted that this is one continuous Greek sentence — 202 words in the original — "the most monstrous sentence congery I know of in Greek" (though he admired Paul's control of it). The structure is doxological, not logical-outline — Paul's grammar is overloaded by praise.
- **Verse 5:** προορίσας ("having predestined") — aorist participle, contemporaneous with or antecedent to the main verb. υἱοθεσίαν ("adoption") — a Roman legal term for taking a child not one's own into full family standing.
- **Verse 6:** ἐχαρίτωσεν ("he hath made us accepted" KJV / "freely bestowed" / "graced") — aorist of χάριτόω, cognate with χάρις (grace), found only here and Luke 1:28 in the NT.
- **Verse 7:** ἀπολύτρωσιν ("redemption") — from ἀπό + λύτρον (ransom), deliverance by payment. ἄφεσιν τῶν παραπτωμάτων ("forgiveness of trespasses") — ἄφεσις is release, letting go.
- **Verse 9:** μυστήριον ("mystery") — in Paul's usage, a truth once hidden in God's counsel, now revealed (not something inherently incomprehensible).
- **Verse 10:** ἀνακεφαλαιώσασθαι ("gather together in one" KJV / "sum up" / "head up") — a hapax legomenon in the NT, from κεφαλή (head); to bring everything under one head.
- **Verse 11:** ἐκληρώθημεν ("we have obtained an inheritance" KJV) — the verb can be read as passive "we were made a heritage" (so the RV margin) or middle "we obtained an inheritance." Robertson discusses the ambiguity.
- **Verse 13:** ἐσφραγίσθητε ("ye were sealed") — aorist passive. The seal marks ownership and authenticity. τῷ πνεύματι τῆς ἐπαγγελίας — dative of instrument ("with the Spirit of promise"), the Spirit who is the content of the promise and who guarantees it.
- **Verse 14:** ἀρραβών ("earnest") — a Semitic loanword (Hebrew עֵרָבוֹן, Genesis 38:17–18 LXX), a commercial term for a first instalment that pledges the full payment. The Spirit is both the promise and the guarantee.

### 5.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Pure grammatical-lexical focus — minimal theological synthesis
- Draws attention to Paul's syntax (the 202-word sentence) as itself exegetically significant
- Traces Greek words to their LXX, classical, and koine usage

## 6. Scofield (Scofield Reference Bible Notes)

**Source:** `Scofield/` (this folder) — SWORD module. Licence to be verified.

**Licence:** `[UNVERIFIED — confirm licence status. The Scofield Reference Bible was first published 1909 (copyright renewed 1917); the 1917 edition may be public domain in the US. Confirm.]`

### 6.1 Approach and Emphasis

Scofield brings his dispensational framework and emphasis on systematic theological categories:

- **"Spiritual blessings in heavenly places" (v. 3):** Scofield contrasts the "spiritual" blessings of the Church age with the material, earthly blessings promised to Israel under the covenants. The "heavenly places" (ἐν τοῖς ἐπουρανίοις) is a distinctive Ephesians phrase occurring five times in the letter — it designates the sphere of the believer's present position in Christ.
- **"Chosen in him before the foundation of the world" (v. 4):** Scofield distinguishes between God's sovereign election of individuals to salvation and God's choosing of the Church as a corporate body. He reads "in him" as limiting election to those in Christ.
- **"Having predestinated us unto the adoption" (v. 5):** Predestination concerns the destination (adoption as sons), not the individuals predestined — it's the "what," not the "who," that is in view.
- **"The mystery of his will" (v. 9):** For Scofield, "mystery" (μυστήριον) is a technical term denoting a truth unrevealed in the OT and now disclosed to Paul. The specific mystery in view is the Church as a body comprising Jew and Gentile on equal footing — a truth not found in OT prophecy.
- **"The dispensation of the fulness of times" (v. 10):** Scofield reads οἰκονομία ("dispensation") in his characteristic sense — a distinct administrative period in God's dealings with humanity. The "fulness of times" is the convergence of all preceding dispensations on Christ's work.
- **"In whom also we have obtained an inheritance" (v. 11):** The "we" are Jewish believers (the "first to trust in Christ," v. 12 NRSV), distinct from the "ye also" of v. 13 (Gentile believers). Scofield emphasises this Jew-Gentile distinction as key to understanding the "mystery."
- **"Sealed with that holy Spirit of promise" (v. 13):** The sealing of the Spirit is the divine mark of ownership occurring at the moment of faith. Scofield distinguishes this from the "filling" of the Spirit as a subsequent experience.
- **"The earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession" (v. 14):** The Spirit as "earnest" (ἀρραβών) guarantees the believer's ultimate redemption — the "purchased possession" is the believer's body, awaiting the resurrection.

### 6.2 Key Interpretive Moves

- Consistently distinguishes Israel from the Church
- Reads "mystery" as technical Pauline vocabulary for the Church age
- Emphasises the believer's position "in the heavenlies" as a present reality
- Traces the trinitarian structure: Father's plan, Son's work, Spirit's application

## 7. Comparative Summary

### 7.1 Agreements Across Commentators

- All six recognise the trinitarian structure of the passage (Father vv. 3–6, Son vv. 7–12, Spirit vv. 13–14)
- All note Paul's doxological tone — this is worship before it is theology
- All affirm Christ as the centre of God's eternal purpose
- All treat the Spirit's sealing as a mark of divine ownership and a guarantee of future inheritance

### 7.2 Key Differences

| Issue | Calvin | Clarke | Scofield | Consensus Direction |
|-------|--------|--------|----------|---------------------|
| Election | Unconditional, individual | Corporate, conditional on faith | Corporate, distinguishes Israel/Church | Varies by theological tradition |
| Ἀνακεφαλαιώσασθαι (v. 10) | Recapitulation of all creation in Christ | Cosmic reconciliation | Dispensational convergence | Christ as cosmic centre |
| "We" vs. "Ye" (vv. 11–13) | General believers | General believers | Jewish vs. Gentile believers | Most commentators see the Jew-Gentile distinction |
| Mystery (v. 9) | God's eternal purpose now revealed | The inclusion of the Gentiles | A truth hidden in OT, revealed to Paul | All agree it was hidden, now revealed |

### 7.3 Pericope Boundaries

All six commentators treat Ephesians 1:3–14 as a single unit (a doxology), though they differ on whether it connects more closely to vv. 1–2 (the greeting) or vv. 15–23 (Paul's prayer). The Greek syntax — one continuous sentence — is the strongest argument for treating vv. 3–14 as a unified pericope.
