# Biblical Symbols and Cross-References

## 1 Purpose and Scope

This file provides a structured taxonomy of biblical symbols together with verified intra-canonical cross-references. It is designed to serve as the content-source for a parser aide that identifies symbolic language in a passage and surfaces related texts across the canon.

### 1.1 Provenance

Sourced from standard biblical reference material and Reformed/evangelical study resources. Every chapter-and-verse reference has been checked for basic accuracy; no invented references are included. References flagged `[UNVERIFIED — check before use]` require manual verification against a primary text before use in any compiled output.

### 1.2 Typological and Canonical Principles

- The taxonomy treats the canon as an interconnected whole in which later texts reuse, develop, and fulfill earlier symbolic patterns (cf. Luke 24:27; 1 Corinthians 10:1–11).
- Symbols are listed in their canonical trajectory: creation → fall → covenant history (patriarchs, exodus, monarchy, prophets) → Christ and the new covenant → consummation.
- Where a symbol appears in both Testaments, the canonical progression is noted.

## 2 Major Biblical Symbols with Cross-References

### 2.1 Water

Water is a primordial symbol of chaos, judgment, deliverance, cleansing, and life, running from Genesis to Revelation.

#### 2.1.1 Creation and Primeval Waters

- **Genesis 1:2** — The Spirit of God hovers over the face of the deep (primordial waters of chaos).
- **Genesis 1:6–7** — God separates the waters above from the waters below.

#### 2.1.2 Judgment and Deliverance

- **Genesis 6–9** — The flood as both divine judgment on sin and means of preserving a remnant through the ark.
- **Exodus 14:21–31** — The parting of the Red Sea; Israel passes through waters of death and emerges delivered (typological ground for baptism, cf. 1 Corinthians 10:1–2).
- **Joshua 3:14–17** — Israel crosses the Jordan on dry ground, echoing the Red Sea.

#### 2.1.3 Cleansing and Ritual

- **Leviticus 14:8–9** — Washing with water for ceremonial cleansing.
- **Numbers 19:11–22** — Water of purification from corpse uncleanness.
- **Ezekiel 36:25–27** — "I will sprinkle clean water on you" as promise of spiritual cleansing in the new covenant.

#### 2.1.4 Living Water and the Spirit

- **Psalm 1:3** — The righteous are like a tree planted by streams of water.
- **Isaiah 44:3** — "I will pour water on the thirsty land, and streams on the dry ground; I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring."
- **Jeremiah 2:13** — The Lord as "the fountain of living waters"; Israel has forsaken him for broken cisterns.
- **John 4:10–14** — Jesus offers the Samaritan woman "living water" that becomes "a spring of water welling up to eternal life."
- **John 7:37–39** — "Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water" — identified as the Holy Spirit.
- **Revelation 22:1–2** — The river of the water of life, flowing from the throne of God and the Lamb, with the tree of life on its banks.

### 2.2 Light

Light is a creation-first symbol associated with divine presence, revelation, holiness, and salvation.

#### 2.2.1 Creation and Divine Presence

- **Genesis 1:3–5** — "Let there be light" — light created and separated from darkness before sun and moon.
- **Exodus 13:21–22** — The pillar of fire giving light to Israel by night.
- **Exodus 34:29–35** — Moses' face shines after speaking with God.

#### 2.2.2 The Lord as Light

- **Psalm 27:1** — "The LORD is my light and my salvation."
- **Psalm 119:105** — "Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."
- **Isaiah 60:1–3** — "Arise, shine, for your light has come" — nations drawn to Israel's light (fulfilled in Christ).

#### 2.2.3 Christ and the New Covenant

- **John 1:4–9** — In the Word was life, "and the life was the light of men" — John the Baptist bears witness to the true light.
- **John 8:12** — "I am the light of the world."
- **John 9:5** — "As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world" (context: healing the man born blind).
- **Matthew 5:14–16** — Believers as the light of the world; a city on a hill.

#### 2.2.4 God as Light and the Consummation

- **1 John 1:5** — "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all."
- **Revelation 21:23** — The New Jerusalem has no need of sun or moon — "the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb."
- **Revelation 22:5** — No more night; no need for lamp or sun — the Lord God will be their light.

### 2.3 Lamb

The lamb is a unifying symbol of substitutionary sacrifice, spanning the Passover to the triumphant Lamb of Revelation.

#### 2.3.1 Old Testament Foundations

- **Genesis 22:7–8** — Isaac asks, "Where is the lamb?"; Abraham replies, "God will provide for himself the lamb."
- **Exodus 12:3–13** — The Passover lamb — blood on the doorposts averts judgment; the lamb is eaten by the household.
- **Leviticus 16:15–22** — The sacrificial goat (parallel imagery) on the Day of Atonement; the scapegoat bears sin into the wilderness.

#### 2.3.2 The Suffering Servant

- **Isaiah 53:7** — The Servant is "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent."

#### 2.3.3 Christ as the Lamb

- **John 1:29** — "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" — John the Baptist's identification of Jesus.
- **John 1:36** — "Behold, the Lamb of God!" (repeated testimony).
- **1 Corinthians 5:7** — "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed."
- **1 Peter 1:18–19** — Redeemed "with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot."

#### 2.3.4 The Lamb in Revelation

- **Revelation 5:6, 12** — The Lamb that was slain, standing at the centre of the throne, worthy to open the scroll.
- **Revelation 7:17** — The Lamb at the centre of the throne shepherds the redeemed and guides them to springs of living water.
- **Revelation 19:7–9** — The marriage supper of the Lamb.
- **Revelation 21:22–23** — The Lord God and the Lamb are the temple of the New Jerusalem; the Lamb is its lamp.
- **Revelation 22:1–3** — The river of life flows from the throne of God and the Lamb; the Lamb's servants worship him.

### 2.4 Bread

Bread is a symbol of God's provision, his presence, and ultimately Christ himself as the sustenance of his people.

#### 2.4.1 Old Testament

- **Exodus 16:4–35** — Manna in the wilderness: "bread from heaven" given daily, prefiguring Christ (cf. John 6:31–33).
- **Leviticus 24:5–9** — The bread of the Presence (showbread) — twelve loaves in the tabernacle, representing the twelve tribes before the Lord.

#### 2.4.2 Christ as the Bread of Life

- **Deuteronomy 8:3** — "Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD" (quoted by Jesus in Matthew 4:4).
- **John 6:31–35** — The crowd references manna; Jesus declares, "I am the bread of life."
- **John 6:48–51** — "I am the living bread that came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever."

#### 2.4.3 The Lord's Supper

- **Matthew 26:26** — "Take, eat; this is my body" — Jesus institutes the Lord's Supper at Passover.
- **Mark 14:22** — Parallel account.
- **Luke 22:19** — "This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me."
- **1 Corinthians 11:23–26** — Paul's institution narrative — "as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes."

### 2.5 Blood

Blood is central to the biblical theology of atonement, covenant, and cleansing.

#### 2.5.1 Sacrificial System

- **Leviticus 17:11** — "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls."
- **Exodus 12:7, 13** — The blood of the Passover lamb on the doorposts — "when I see the blood, I will pass over you."
- **Exodus 24:8** — Moses sprinkles the blood of the covenant on the people at Sinai.

#### 2.5.2 The New Covenant in Christ's Blood

- **Matthew 26:28** — "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins."
- **Ephesians 1:7** — "In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses."
- **Hebrews 9:12–14** — Christ entered the holy place "by means of his own blood," securing eternal redemption; contrasts with the blood of goats and bulls.
- **Hebrews 9:22** — "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins."

#### 2.5.3 Redemption, Cleansing, and Victory

- **1 Peter 1:18–19** — Redeemed "with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot."
- **1 John 1:7** — "The blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin."
- **Revelation 12:11** — The saints have conquered the accuser "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony."

### 2.6 Tree and Tree of Life

The tree symbol traces humanity's fall, the life of the righteous, the cross, and eschatological restoration.

#### 2.6.1 Eden

- **Genesis 2:9** — The tree of life in the midst of the garden, alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
- **Genesis 2:16–17** — The prohibition concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
- **Genesis 3:22–24** — Humanity is expelled from Eden and barred from the tree of life by the cherubim with the flaming sword.

#### 2.6.2 The Righteous as a Tree

- **Psalm 1:3** — The blessed man is "like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season."
- **Jeremiah 17:7–8** — The one who trusts in the Lord is "like a tree planted by water."
- **Psalm 92:12–14** — "The righteous flourish like the palm tree… planted in the house of the LORD."

#### 2.6.3 The Cross as Tree

- **Deuteronomy 21:22–23** — "Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree" (applied to Christ's crucifixion in Galatians 3:13).
- **Acts 5:30** — Peter preaches Christ, "whom you killed by hanging him on a tree."
- **Acts 10:39** — "They put him to death by hanging him on a tree."
- **Galatians 3:13** — "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us — for it is written, 'Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree.'"
- **1 Peter 2:24** — "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree."

#### 2.6.4 Eschatological Tree of Life

- **Proverbs 3:18** — Wisdom is "a tree of life to those who lay hold of her."
- **Revelation 2:7** — "To the one who conquers I will grant to eat of the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God."
- **Revelation 22:2, 14** — The tree of life in the New Jerusalem, bearing twelve kinds of fruit, its leaves "for the healing of the nations."
- **Revelation 22:19** — Warning against taking away from the words of the prophecy, resulting in loss of share in the tree of life.

### 2.7 Serpent and Dragon

The serpent is the archetypal adversary, originating in Eden and culminating in the apocalyptic dragon of Revelation.

#### 2.7.1 The Fall

- **Genesis 3:1–15** — The serpent deceives Eve; God pronounces enmity between the serpent and the woman, and between their offspring (the Protoevangelium in v.15).
- **Genesis 3:14** — The serpent is cursed to crawl on its belly.

#### 2.7.2 Bronze Serpent — Judgment and Healing

- **Numbers 21:4–9** — Fiery serpents sent as judgment; the bronze serpent lifted on a pole so that looking upon it brings healing.
- **John 3:14–15** — Jesus interprets the bronze serpent: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up."

#### 2.7.3 Dragon/Satan in Revelation

- **Revelation 12:3–4, 7–9** — The great red dragon identified as "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan"; cast down from heaven.
- **Revelation 12:13–17** — The dragon pursues the woman and makes war on her offspring.
- **Revelation 20:2** — The angel seizes "the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan" and binds him for a thousand years.
- **Revelation 20:10** — The devil is thrown into the lake of fire.

#### 2.7.4 Apostolic Warning

- **2 Corinthians 11:3** — Paul warns: "as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts will be led astray."
- **Romans 16:20** — "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet" (echoing Genesis 3:15).

### 2.8 Temple and Tabernacle

The dwelling place of God with his people moves from portable tent to stone temple to the incarnate Christ to the church to the new creation.

#### 2.8.1 Tabernacle

- **Exodus 25–31** — Detailed instructions for the tabernacle, given on Sinai.
- **Exodus 35–40** — Construction and erection; the glory of the Lord fills the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34–38).
- **Leviticus** — The sacrificial system and priestly service centred on the tabernacle.
- **Numbers 2** — The camp of Israel arranged around the tabernacle at the centre.

#### 2.8.2 The Temple in Jerusalem

- **2 Samuel 7:1–13** — God's promise to David concerning a house (dynasty) and the son who will build a house (temple) for the Lord.
- **1 Kings 6–8** — Solomon builds and dedicates the temple; the glory of the Lord fills it (1 Kings 8:10–11).
- **Ezekiel 10:18–19** — A vision of the glory departing from the temple before exile.
- **Ezekiel 40–48** — Vision of a restored, eschatological temple.
- **Haggai 2:9** — "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."

#### 2.8.3 Jesus as the Temple

- **John 1:14** — "The Word became flesh and dwelt [tabernacled] among us, and we have seen his glory."
- **John 2:19–21** — "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" — "he was speaking about the temple of his body."
- **Matthew 12:6** — "I tell you, something greater than the temple is here."

#### 2.8.4 Believers as the Temple

- **1 Corinthians 3:16–17** — "You [plural] are God's temple and … God's Spirit dwells in you."
- **1 Corinthians 6:19–20** — "Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you."
- **Ephesians 2:19–22** — The church as a holy temple in the Lord, "a dwelling place for God by the Spirit."
- **1 Peter 2:4–5** — Believers as living stones being built into "a spiritual house."

#### 2.8.5 Consummation

- **Revelation 21:22** — "I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb."
- **Revelation 21:3** — "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man" — the goal of the entire temple trajectory.

### 2.9 Vine and Vineyard

Israel is portrayed as God's vine/vineyard; Jesus claims to be the true vine; fruitfulness depends on abiding in him.

#### 2.9.1 Israel as the Vine/Vineyard

- **Isaiah 5:1–7** — The song of the vineyard: the Lord plants and tends his vineyard (Israel) but it yields wild grapes; judgment follows.
- **Psalm 80:8–16** — Israel as a vine brought out of Egypt and planted in the land, now broken down.
- **Jeremiah 2:21** — "I planted you a choice vine, wholly of pure seed. How then have you turned degenerate?"
- **Ezekiel 15:1–8** — The vine-wood parable: its worth is only as fuel for fire if it bears no fruit.

#### 2.9.2 Christ as the True Vine

- **John 15:1** — "I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser."
- **John 15:4–5** — "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself … I am the vine; you are the branches."

#### 2.9.3 Vineyard Parables

- **Matthew 20:1–16** — The labourers in the vineyard (the kingdom and divine generosity).
- **Matthew 21:33–46** — The parable of the tenants in the vineyard (echoes Isaiah 5; the tenants kill the son).
- **Mark 12:1–12** — Parallel account of the tenants.
- **Luke 20:9–19** — Parallel account.

#### 2.9.4 Eschatological Imagery

- **Genesis 49:11** — Jacob's blessing: "binding his foal to the vine … he has washed his garments in wine" — messianic abundance.
- **Revelation 14:18–20** — The angel gathers the clusters of the vine of the earth for the great winepress of God's wrath.

### 2.10 Bride and Bridegroom

The marriage metaphor depicts God's covenant love for his people, consummated at the return of Christ.

#### 2.10.1 Old Testament Foundations

- **Hosea 2:19–20** — "I will betroth you to me forever … in righteousness and in justice, in steadfast love and in mercy."
- **Isaiah 54:5** — "Your Maker is your husband" — the Lord as the husband of his people.
- **Isaiah 62:5** — "As the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you."
- **Song of Songs** — Poetic celebration of marital love, read canonically as depicting the love between Christ and the church [UNVERIFIED — the Christological reading is a received interpretive tradition, not an explicit New Testament assertion].
- **Ezekiel 16** — Extended allegory of Jerusalem as an unfaithful wife (the negative image).

#### 2.10.2 New Testament

- **Matthew 9:15** — John the Baptist's disciples; Jesus speaks of himself as the bridegroom.
- **John 3:29** — John the Baptist says, "The one who has the bride is the bridegroom."
- **Ephesians 5:25–33** — The "great mystery" of marriage referring to Christ and the church — Christ loved the church and gave himself for her.
- **2 Corinthians 11:2** — Paul betrothed the Corinthian church "to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ."

#### 2.10.3 The Marriage Supper of the Lamb

- **Revelation 19:7–9** — "The marriage of the Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready."
- **Revelation 21:2** — The holy city, the New Jerusalem, "prepared as a bride adorned for her husband."
- **Revelation 21:9** — "Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb" — the angel shows John the New Jerusalem.
- **Revelation 22:17** — "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.'"

### 2.11 Lion

The lion symbolises royal authority (Lion of Judah) and, in a contrasting use, predatory danger (Satan).

#### 2.11.1 The Lion of Judah

- **Genesis 49:9–10** — Jacob's blessing: "Judah is a lion's cub … the scepter shall not depart from Judah."
- **Numbers 24:9** — Balaam's oracle: "He crouched, he lay down like a lion."

#### 2.11.2 Messianic Fulfilment

- **Revelation 5:5** — "Weep no more; behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered." (The one who opens the scroll is then revealed as the slain Lamb — combining the two images.)

#### 2.11.3 Satan as a Roaring Lion

- **1 Peter 5:8** — "Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour."
- **2 Timothy 4:17** — Paul was "rescued from the lion's mouth" [UNVERIFIED — "lion's mouth" could refer to literal execution or a metaphorical enemy; scholarly debate].

### 2.12 Rock

The rock symbolises stability, refuge, and the life-giving presence of God in Christ.

#### 2.12.1 God as Rock

- **Deuteronomy 32:4** — "The Rock, his work is perfect."
- **Deuteronomy 32:15** — Israel "forsook God who made him and scoffed at the Rock of his salvation."
- **Psalm 18:2** — "The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer."
- **Psalm 62:1–2** — "He only is my rock and my salvation."

#### 2.12.2 Water from the Rock

- **Exodus 17:1–7** — Water from the rock at Massah and Meribah; Moses strikes the rock.
- **Numbers 20:1–13** — Water from the rock at Kadesh; Moses strikes the rock instead of speaking to it; his disobedience prevents him from entering the land.

#### 2.12.3 Christ as the Rock

- **1 Corinthians 10:4** — "They drank from the spiritual Rock that followed them, and the Rock was Christ."
- **Matthew 16:18** — "On this rock I will build my church" — the confession of Christ as the Son of God, or Peter as the confessor [UNVERIFIED — interpretation is debated; both views exist within evangelical scholarship].
- **Romans 9:33** — A stumbling stone and rock of offence (quoting Isaiah 8:14; 28:16).
- **1 Peter 2:4–8** — Christ as the living stone, rejected by men but chosen by God — the cornerstone.

#### 2.12.4 The Wise Builder

- **Matthew 7:24–25** — The wise man builds his house on the rock (obedience to Christ's words).

### 2.13 Fire

Fire symbolises the holy presence of God, his word, judgment, refining, and the empowering Spirit.

#### 2.13.1 Divine Presence

- **Exodus 3:2–6** — The angel of the Lord appears to Moses in the burning bush — fire that does not consume.
- **Exodus 13:21–22** — The pillar of fire guiding Israel.
- **Exodus 19:18** — Mount Sinai wrapped in smoke "because the LORD had descended on it in fire."
- **Exodus 24:17** — The appearance of the glory of the Lord was like a devouring fire.

#### 2.13.2 God as Consuming Fire

- **Deuteronomy 4:24** — "The LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God."
- **Hebrews 12:28–29** — "Let us offer to God acceptable worship … for our God is a consuming fire."
- **Isaiah 66:15–16** — The Lord will come in fire to execute judgment.

#### 2.13.3 The Spirit as Fire

- **Matthew 3:11** — John the Baptist: "He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire."
- **Acts 2:3–4** — Tongues of fire on the disciples at Pentecost, filled with the Holy Spirit.

#### 2.13.4 Refining Fire

- **Malachi 3:2–3** — The Lord is "like a refiner's fire" — he will purify the sons of Levi.
- **Zechariah 13:9** — "I will refine them as one refines silver."
- **1 Peter 1:6–7** — Faith tested by fire "may be found to result in praise and glory and honour."
- **1 Corinthians 3:13–15** — Each one's work will be tested by fire on the day of judgment.

#### 2.13.5 Word as Fire

- **Jeremiah 23:29** — "Is not my word like fire, declares the LORD?"
- **Jeremiah 20:9** — The word is "a burning fire shut up in my bones."

### 2.14 Door and Gate

Doors and gates symbolise access to safety, the presence of God, and entry into the kingdom.

#### 2.14.1 Passover Door

- **Exodus 12:7, 22–23** — Blood applied to the doorposts and lintel; the Lord passes over the door and does not allow the destroyer to enter.
- **Exodus 12:23** — "The LORD will pass over the door."

#### 2.14.2 Christ as the Door

- **John 10:7** — "I am the door of the sheep."
- **John 10:9** — "I am the door. If anyone enters by me, he will be saved."
- **John 14:6** — "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (related imagery).

#### 2.14.3 The Narrow Gate

- **Matthew 7:13–14** — "Enter by the narrow gate. For the gate is wide and the way is easy that leads to destruction … the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life."
- **Luke 13:24** — "Strive to enter through the narrow door."

#### 2.14.4 Eschatological Gates

- **Psalm 24:7–10** — "Lift up your heads, O gates! … that the King of glory may come in."
- **Revelation 21:12–13** — The New Jerusalem has twelve gates, three on each side, inscribed with the names of the twelve tribes.
- **Revelation 21:21** — The twelve gates are twelve pearls.
- **Revelation 21:25** — Its gates will never be shut by day — "and there will be no night there."
- **Revelation 22:14** — "Blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and that they may enter the city by the gates."

### 2.15 Shepherd

The shepherd motif runs from Abel through David to Christ, the Good Shepherd and Chief Shepherd.

#### 2.15.1 The Lord as Shepherd

- **Psalm 23:1–6** — "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."
- **Psalm 80:1** — "Give ear, O Shepherd of Israel."
- **Isaiah 40:11** — "He will tend his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs in his arms."
- **Ezekiel 34:11–16** — The Lord himself will search for his sheep and tend them, in contrast to Israel's faithless shepherds.
- **Ezekiel 34:23–24** — "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David."

#### 2.15.2 Christ as the Good Shepherd

- **John 10:11** — "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
- **John 10:14–16** — "I know my own and my own know me … and I have other sheep that are not of this fold."
- **Hebrews 13:20** — "The great shepherd of the sheep" raised from the dead.
- **1 Peter 2:25** — "You were straying like sheep, but have now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls."

#### 2.15.3 Chief Shepherd and Under-Shepherds

- **1 Peter 5:2–4** — Elders exhorted to shepherd the flock willingly; "when the chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown of glory."
- **Acts 20:28** — Paul instructs Ephesian elders to "care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood."

#### 2.15.4 Eschatological Shepherd

- **Matthew 25:32–33** — The Son of Man separates the sheep from the goats at the final judgment.
- **Revelation 7:17** — "The Lamb in the midst of the throne will be their shepherd, and he will guide them to springs of living water."

### 2.16 Seed

Seed represents offspring, covenant promise, the word of God, and the kingdom's growth.

#### 2.16.1 The Protoevangelium

- **Genesis 3:15** — "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring [seed] and her offspring [seed]; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."

#### 2.16.2 Abrahamic Promise

- **Genesis 12:7** — "To your offspring [seed] I will give this land."
- **Genesis 15:5** — "Look toward heaven, and number the stars … So shall your offspring be."
- **Genesis 22:17–18** — "I will surely multiply your offspring as the stars of heaven … in your offspring shall all the nations of the earth be blessed."

#### 2.16.3 Christ as the Seed

- **Galatians 3:16** — "Now the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' who is Christ."
- **Galatians 3:29** — "If you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise."

#### 2.16.4 Parable of the Sower

- **Matthew 13:3–9, 18–23** — The sower, the seed (the word of the kingdom), and the four soils.
- **Mark 4:3–9, 13–20** — Parallel account.
- **Luke 8:5–8, 11–15** — Parallel account.

#### 2.16.5 The Mustard Seed

- **Matthew 13:31–32** — The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed — the smallest of seeds becomes a tree.

### 2.17 Garment and Robe

Garments represent righteousness or its lack, covering for shame, priestly mediation, and eschatological purity.

#### 2.17.1 Covering Shame

- **Genesis 3:7, 21** — Adam and Eve sew fig leaves; the Lord clothes them with garments of skin (the first covering for sin, requiring a death).

#### 2.17.2 Priestly Garments

- **Exodus 28:1–43** — Detailed instructions for the high priest's garments, including the ephod, breastpiece, robe, and turban with "Holy to the LORD."
- **Leviticus 8:7–9** — Aaron is clothed in the priestly garments at ordination.

#### 2.17.3 Righteousness as a Garment

- **Isaiah 61:10** — "He has clothed me with the garments of salvation; he has covered me with the robe of righteousness."
- **Zechariah 3:3–5** — Joshua the high priest stands before the angel in filthy garments; they are removed, and he is clothed with pure vestments ("I have taken your iniquity away from you").
- **Job 29:14** — "I put on righteousness, and it clothed me."

#### 2.17.4 New Covenant Imagery

- **Romans 13:14** — "Put on the Lord Jesus Christ."
- **Galatians 3:27** — "As many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ."
- **Colossians 3:12** — "Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience."

#### 2.17.5 White Robes

- **Revelation 3:5** — The one who conquers will be clothed in white garments; Christ will not blot his name from the book of life.
- **Revelation 6:11** — Martyrs are given white robes.
- **Revelation 7:9** — The great multitude stands before the throne "clothed in white robes, with palm branches in their hands."
- **Revelation 7:13–14** — "They have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb."
- **Revelation 19:8** — The Bride is granted "to clothe herself with fine linen, bright and pure" — "the righteous deeds of the saints."

### 2.18 Oil and Anointing

Oil signifies consecration for office, the Holy Spirit, healing, and joy.

#### 2.18.1 Anointing in the Old Testament

- **Exodus 30:22–33** — The recipe for the holy anointing oil, reserved for the tabernacle, priests, and sacred objects.
- **1 Samuel 10:1** — Samuel anoints Saul as king with oil.
- **1 Samuel 16:13** — Samuel anoints David; "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward" — linking oil with the Spirit's empowerment.
- **1 Kings 19:16** — Elijah instructed to anoint Elisha as prophet.
- **Psalm 133:2** — The precious oil running down Aaron's beard — a picture of unity and consecration.

#### 2.18.2 Messianic Anointing

- **Psalm 2:2** — The kings of the earth set themselves against "the LORD and against his Anointed" (Messiah/Christ).
- **Isaiah 61:1** — "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me" — read by Jesus in the synagogue at Nazareth.
- **Luke 4:18–21** — Jesus reads Isaiah 61 and declares, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing."
- **Acts 10:38** — "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power."

#### 2.18.3 The Anointing of Believers

- **2 Corinthians 1:21–22** — God "has anointed us, and who has also put his seal on us and given us his Spirit."
- **1 John 2:20** — "You have been anointed by the Holy One, and you all have knowledge."
- **1 John 2:27** — "The anointing that you received from him abides in you."
- **Zechariah 4:2–6** — The vision of the golden lampstand and the two olive trees supplying oil: "'Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit,' says the LORD of hosts."

#### 2.18.4 Oil in Healing and Mercy

- **Mark 6:13** — The disciples "anointed with oil many who were sick and healed them."
- **James 5:14** — Elders are to pray over the sick, "anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord."
- **Luke 10:34** — The Good Samaritan pours oil and wine on the wounds of the beaten man.

## 3 Secondary Symbols

These symbols carry significant theological weight and appear in multiple canonical contexts, but have a narrower canonical range or function more often as subsidiary imagery to the major symbols above.

### 3.1 Dove

- **Genesis 8:8–12** — Noah sends out a dove from the ark three times; it returns with an olive leaf, signalling the receding of the flood waters and peace.
- **Song of Songs 2:14** — The beloved is called "my dove."
- **Matthew 3:16** — At Jesus' baptism, the Spirit of God descends "like a dove" and alights on him.
- **Mark 1:10** — Parallel: the Spirit descending like a dove.
- **Luke 3:22** — Parallel; the Holy Spirit descends "in bodily form, like a dove."
- **John 1:32** — John the Baptist testifies: "I saw the Spirit descend from heaven like a dove, and it remained on him."

**Symbolism:** The Holy Spirit; peace; purity; new creation after judgment (the flood context).

### 3.2 Ark

The ark symbol operates in two primary canonical forms: the vessel of salvation and the throne of God's presence.

#### 3.2.1 Noah's Ark

- **Genesis 6:13–22** — God commands Noah to build the ark; instructions for its dimensions and construction.
- **Genesis 7:1–24** — The flood; Noah, his family, and the animals enter the ark.
- **1 Peter 3:20–21** — The flood as a type of baptism; "God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water."

#### 3.2.2 Ark of the Covenant

- **Exodus 25:10–22** — Precise instructions for the ark with the mercy seat; "there I will meet with you."
- **Exodus 37:1–9** — The construction of the ark by Bezalel.
- **Joshua 3–4** — The ark leads Israel across the Jordan.
- **Joshua 6** — The ark is carried around Jericho; the walls fall.
- **1 Samuel 4–6** — The ark captured by the Philistines; Dagon falls; the ark returns.
- **2 Samuel 6** — David brings the ark to Jerusalem.
- **1 Kings 8:1–11** — The ark placed in Solomon's temple.

#### 3.2.3 The Ark in Eschatology

- **Revelation 11:19** — "Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple."
- **Hebrews 9:3–5** — The ark described as part of the earthly tabernacle, which was "a copy and shadow of the heavenly things" (Hebrews 8:5).

### 3.3 Rainbow

- **Genesis 9:12–17** — God sets the rainbow in the clouds as the sign of the Noahic covenant — a promise never to destroy the earth again by flood.
- **Ezekiel 1:28** — The appearance of the likeness of the glory of the Lord is "like the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud on the day of rain."
- **Revelation 4:3** — A rainbow that looks like an emerald encircles the throne in heaven.
- **Revelation 10:1** — A mighty angel with a rainbow over his head.

**Symbolism:** Covenant faithfulness; divine mercy; the glory of God's presence; the merging of judgment and mercy.

### 3.4 Manna

- **Exodus 16:13–36** — Manna provided daily in the wilderness for forty years; instructions for gathering and keeping (an omer placed before the Lord as a memorial).
- **Numbers 11:7–9** — Description of manna: like coriander seed, appearance like bdellium.
- **Deuteronomy 8:3, 16** — Manna as divine provision, humbling Israel and teaching dependence on God's word.
- **Joshua 5:12** — Manna ceases the day after Israel eats the produce of the promised land.
- **Nehemiah 9:20** — Recounting: "You gave your good Spirit to instruct them and did not withhold your manna from their mouth."

#### 3.4.1 Christ as the True Manna

- **John 6:30–35** — The crowd asks for a sign like manna; Jesus responds, "I am the bread of life."
- **John 6:48–51** — "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died … I am the living bread that came down from heaven."
- **Revelation 2:17** — To the one who conquers, Christ will give "some of the hidden manna."

### 3.5 Rod and Staff

- **Exodus 4:2–4, 17** — Moses' shepherd's staff becomes the "staff of God," used for signs (turning to a serpent, turning back).
- **Exodus 7:9–12** — Aaron's staff becomes a serpent before Pharaoh.
- **Exodus 14:16** — Moses stretches out his staff over the Red Sea to part the waters.
- **Exodus 17:5–6** — Moses strikes the rock with his staff; water flows.
- **Numbers 17:1–11** — Aaron's staff buds, blossoms, and bears almonds, confirming his priesthood.
- **Psalm 23:4** — "Your rod and your staff, they comfort me."
- **Psalm 2:9** — The Messianic king will "break them with a rod of iron" (cited in Revelation 2:27; 12:5; 19:15).
- **Isaiah 11:1, 4** — "There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse" (branch/rod imagery); "he shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth."

**Symbolism:** Divine authority; shepherding care and discipline; miraculous power; the word as instrument of judgment and rule.

### 3.6 Sword

- **Genesis 3:24** — The cherubim with the flaming sword guard the way to the tree of life.
- **Deuteronomy 32:41** — "I sharpen my flashing sword" (divine judgment).
- **Psalm 149:6** — High praises of God in their throats and a "two-edged sword" in their hands.
- **Isaiah 49:2** — The Servant's mouth is "like a sharp sword" (divine word).
- **Matthew 10:34** — "I have not come to bring peace, but a sword" — the dividing effect of Christ's message.

#### 3.6.1 The Word as Sword

- **Ephesians 6:17** — "The sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (the one offensive piece of the armour of God).
- **Hebrews 4:12** — "The word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit."
- **Revelation 1:16** — From Christ's mouth comes "a sharp two-edged sword."
- **Revelation 19:15** — "From his mouth comes a sharp sword with which to strike down the nations."
- **Revelation 19:21** — The rest are slain by the sword that comes from the mouth of the rider on the horse.

### 3.7 Seal

- **Song of Songs 8:6** — "Set me as a seal upon your heart" — love as an unbreakable mark of belonging.
- **Isaiah 8:16** — "Bind up the testimony; seal the teaching among my disciples."
- **Jeremiah 32:10–14** — Jeremiah seals the deed of purchase as a sign of future restoration.
- **Daniel 6:17** — A stone and the king's seal on the lion's den — irrevocable decree.
- **Daniel 12:4** — "Seal the book until the time of the end."

#### 3.7.1 New Testament Sealing

- **John 6:27** — "On him God the Father has set his seal."
- **2 Corinthians 1:21–22** — God "has also put his seal on us and given us his Spirit in our hearts as a guarantee."
- **Ephesians 1:13–14** — Believers are "sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance."
- **Ephesians 4:30** — "Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption."

#### 3.7.2 Apocalyptic Seals

- **Revelation 5:1–5** — The scroll sealed with seven seals; only the Lamb is worthy to open it.
- **Revelation 6–8:1** — The opening of the seven seals.
- **Revelation 7:3–4** — The servants of God sealed on their foreheads (144,000 from the tribes of Israel).
- **Revelation 9:4** — The locusts are told to harm only those without the seal of God.
- **Revelation 10:4** — "Seal up what the seven thunders have said."

**Symbolism:** Ownership; authenticity; security and preservation; the Holy Spirit's guarantee; divine decree.

### 3.8 Trumpet

- **Exodus 19:16, 19** — A loud trumpet blast at Sinai: the presence of God on the mountain, growing louder and louder.
- **Leviticus 25:9** — The trumpet sound on the Day of Atonement in the Year of Jubilee, proclaiming liberty.
- **Numbers 10:1–10** — The two silver trumpets used for summoning the congregation and for battle.
- **Joshua 6:4–20** — Seven priests blow seven trumpets of rams' horns for seven days; Jericho's walls fall.

#### 3.8.1 Prophetic and Eschatological Trumpet

- **Joel 2:1** — "Blow a trumpet in Zion; sound an alarm on my holy mountain!" — the day of the Lord.
- **Isaiah 27:13** — "A great trumpet will be blown, and those who were lost in the land of Assyria … shall come and worship the LORD."
- **Zephaniah 1:16** — "A day of trumpet blast and battle cry against the fortified cities."

#### 3.8.2 New Testament and Apocalyptic

- **Matthew 24:31** — The Son of Man "will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect from the four winds."
- **1 Corinthians 15:52** — "In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable."
- **1 Thessalonians 4:16** — "The Lord himself will descend from heaven … with the sound of the trumpet of God."
- **Hebrews 12:19** — Sinai: "the sound of a trumpet and a voice whose words made the hearers beg that no further messages be spoken to them."

#### 3.8.3 The Seven Trumpets

- **Revelation 8:2** — Seven trumpets given to the seven angels.
- **Revelation 8:6–13** — The first four trumpets sound.
- **Revelation 9:1–21** — The fifth and sixth trumpets (the first two woes).
- **Revelation 11:15** — The seventh trumpet: the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ.

**Symbolism:** Divine summons and warning; the proclamation of the gospel; final judgment; the resurrection; the arrival of God's kingdom.

### 3.9 Cloud

- **Exodus 13:21–22** — The pillar of cloud by day and pillar of fire by night guide Israel.
- **Exodus 14:19–20** — The cloud moves behind Israel, separating them from the Egyptian army.
- **Exodus 16:10** — The glory of the Lord appears in the cloud in the wilderness.
- **Exodus 19:9** — The Lord descends on Sinai in a thick cloud.
- **Exodus 24:15–18** — The cloud covers Sinai for six days; Moses enters the cloud on the seventh and remains forty days.
- **Exodus 33:9–10** — The pillar of cloud descends at the tent of meeting.
- **Exodus 40:34–38** — The cloud covers the tent of meeting; the glory of the Lord fills the tabernacle; Israel travels when the cloud lifts.

#### 3.9.1 The Cloud of God's Presence

- **1 Kings 8:10–11** — The cloud fills the temple at its dedication.
- **Leviticus 16:2** — The Lord will appear in the cloud over the mercy seat on the Day of Atonement.
- **Isaiah 19:1** — The Lord rides on a swift cloud.

#### 3.9.2 New Testament Cloud Imagery

- **Matthew 17:5** — At the transfiguration, "a bright cloud overshadowed them, and a voice from the cloud said, 'This is my beloved Son … listen to him.'"
- **Mark 9:7** — Parallel.
- **Luke 9:34–35** — Parallel.
- **Acts 1:9** — Jesus "was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight" — the ascension.

#### 3.9.3 Eschatological Cloud

- **Matthew 24:30** — The Son of Man coming "on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory" (citing Daniel 7:13).
- **Mark 13:26** — Parallel.
- **Revelation 1:7** — "He is coming with the clouds."
- **Revelation 10:1** — A mighty angel wrapped in a cloud.
- **Revelation 11:12** — The two witnesses are taken up to heaven in a cloud.
- **Revelation 14:14–16** — One like a son of man seated on a cloud with a sickle.

**Symbolism:** The veiled glory and presence of God; divine guidance; judgment; hidden mystery; the ascension and return of Christ.

### 3.10 Wilderness

- **Exodus through Deuteronomy** — Israel's forty years in the wilderness: a place of testing, provision, covenant formation, and rebellion.
- **Numbers 14:33–34** — The forty years correspond to forty days of spying out the land.
- **Deuteronomy 8:2–5** — The wilderness as divine discipline, testing, and fatherly training.
- **Isaiah 40:3** — "In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD; make straight in the desert a highway for our God."
- **Hosea 2:14** — God allures Israel into the wilderness to speak tenderly to her.

#### 3.10.1 New Testament Wilderness

- **Matthew 4:1–11** — Jesus led by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted; he overcomes where Israel failed (citing Deuteronomy in each response).
- **Mark 1:12–13** — Parallel; Jesus with the wild animals.
- **Luke 4:1–13** — Parallel.
- **Revelation 12:6, 14** — The woman flees to the wilderness, where she is nourished by God for 1,260 days, protected from the dragon.
- **Hebrews 3:7–19** — Warning against hardening hearts as Israel did in the wilderness, failing to enter God's rest.

**Symbolism:** Testing and trial; dependence on God; preparation; divine encounter; refuge from persecution.

### 3.11 Mountain (Sinai and Zion)

- **Exodus 3:1** — Horeb, "the mountain of God" — Moses encounters the burning bush.
- **Exodus 19–24** — Mount Sinai: the giving of the law, the covenant ratified, the elders see God.
- **Exodus 24:17** — The glory of the Lord like a devouring fire on the top of Sinai.
- **1 Kings 19:8–18** — Elijah at Horeb/Sinai: the wind, earthquake, and fire, then "a low whisper."

#### 3.11.1 Mount Zion

- **Psalm 2:6** — "As for me, I have set my King on Zion, my holy hill."
- **Psalm 48:1–2** — Mount Zion, "the city of the great King."
- **Isaiah 2:2–4** — The mountain of the house of the Lord shall be established as the highest mountain; nations shall flow to it (cf. Micah 4:1–3).
- **Isaiah 25:6–9** — On this mountain, the Lord will swallow up death forever; a feast for all peoples.

#### 3.11.2 New Testament and Eschatological Mountains

- **Matthew 5:1–2** — The Sermon on the Mount: Jesus as the new lawgiver.
- **Matthew 17:1–8** — The Mount of Transfiguration.
- **Hebrews 12:18–24** — "You have not come to what may be touched" (Sinai) but "to Mount Zion … the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem."
- **Revelation 14:1** — The Lamb standing on Mount Zion with the 144,000.
- **Revelation 21:10** — John is carried "to a great, high mountain" to see the holy city, the New Jerusalem.

**Symbolism:** Divine revelation and law; the place of God's special presence; the church (heavenly Zion); the new creation.

### 3.12 Day and Night

- **Genesis 1:3–5** — "God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night" — the fundamental created order.
- **Exodus 10:21–23** — The plague of darkness over Egypt, while Israel had light.

#### 3.12.1 Ethical and Eschatological Dualism

- **Isaiah 60:19–20** — "The sun shall be no more your light by day … the LORD will be your everlasting light."
- **John 9:4** — "We must work the works of him who sent me while it is day; night is coming, when no one can work."
- **John 11:9–10** — Walking in the day vs. stumbling in the night.
- **Romans 13:11–12** — "The night is far gone; the day is at hand."
- **1 Thessalonians 5:5–8** — "You are all children of light, children of the day. We are not of the night or of the darkness."
- **Revelation 21:25** — The New Jerusalem's gates never shut by day — "and there will be no night there."
- **Revelation 22:5** — "Night will be no more. They will need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light."

**Symbolism:** The coming of the new creation in which darkness, sin, and death are abolished; the present age as a time of mission before judgment.

### 3.13 Harvest

- **Leviticus 23:22** — The law of gleaning; provision for the poor at harvest.
- **Ruth 2** — The narrative of gleaning during the barley harvest.

#### 3.13.1 Harvest as Judgment and Mission

- **Isaiah 17:5** — Harvest imagery for judgment.
- **Joel 3:13** — "Put in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe" — judgment on the nations.
- **Matthew 9:37–38** — "The harvest is plentiful, but the labourers are few; therefore pray earnestly to the Lord of the harvest."
- **Matthew 13:30, 39** — The harvest at "the end of the age" in the parable of the wheat and tares.
- **John 4:35–38** — "Look, I tell you, the fields are white for harvest."
- **Galatians 6:9** — "Let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up."

#### 3.13.2 Eschatological Harvest

- **Revelation 14:14–16** — One like a son of man reaps the earth with a sickle.
- **Revelation 14:17–20** — A second angel gathers the vine of the earth for the great winepress of God's wrath.

**Symbolism:** Mission and evangelism; the gathering of souls into the kingdom; final judgment; the separation of righteous and wicked.

### 3.14 Firstfruits

- **Exodus 23:19** — "The best of the firstfruits of your ground you shall bring into the house of the LORD your God."
- **Exodus 34:26** — Repeated instruction.
- **Leviticus 23:9–14** — The wave offering of the firstfruits of the barley harvest.
- **Numbers 18:12** — The firstfruits are given to the priests.

#### 3.14.1 Christ as Firstfruits

- **1 Corinthians 15:20** — "Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep."
- **1 Corinthians 15:23** — "Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to Christ."

#### 3.14.2 Believers and the Spirit as Firstfruits

- **Romans 8:23** — Believers "have the firstfruits of the Spirit" as they groan and await adoption.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:13** — God chose the Thessalonians "as the firstfruits to be saved."
- **James 1:18** — Believers are "a kind of firstfruits of his creatures."
- **Revelation 14:4** — The 144,000 are "redeemed from mankind as firstfruits for God and the Lamb."

**Symbolism:** Christ's resurrection as the guarantee of the general resurrection; believers as the beginning of the new creation; the Holy Spirit as the down-payment of future glory.

### 3.15 Cup

- **Genesis 40:11** — Pharaoh's cupbearer's dream: pressing grapes into Pharaoh's cup.
- **Psalm 23:5** — "My cup overflows" — abundant blessing.
- **Psalm 75:8** — "In the hand of the LORD there is a cup with foaming wine … all the wicked of the earth shall drain it down" — the cup of divine wrath.

#### 3.15.1 The Cup of Wrath

- **Isaiah 51:17, 22** — Jerusalem has drunk "the cup of his wrath"; the Lord takes it from her hand.
- **Jeremiah 25:15–29** — The cup of the wine of wrath: Jeremiah is commanded to make all the nations drink it.
- **Ezekiel 23:32–34** — The cup of horror and desolation for Samaria.

#### 3.15.2 Christ's Cup

- **Matthew 20:22–23** — "Are you able to drink the cup that I am to drink?" — the cup of suffering.
- **Matthew 26:39** — "If it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will."
- **Matthew 26:42** — "If this cannot pass unless I drink it, your will be done" (cf. John 18:11 — "shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?").

#### 3.15.3 The Cup of the Lord's Supper

- **1 Corinthians 10:16** — "The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood of Christ?"
- **1 Corinthians 11:25** — "This cup is the new covenant in my blood."

#### 3.15.4 The Cup in Revelation

- **Revelation 14:10** — Those who worship the beast will "drink the wine of God's wrath, poured full strength into the cup of his anger."
- **Revelation 16:19** — Babylon is made to drain the cup of the wine of the fury of God's wrath.
- **Revelation 17:4** — Babylon holds a golden cup full of abominations.
- **Revelation 18:6** — "In the cup she has mixed, mix her a double portion."

**Symbolism:** Blessing and overflowing provision; Christ's suffering and substitutionary death; the Lord's Supper; divine wrath on sin; judgment on the enemies of God.

### 3.16 Throne

- **Isaiah 6:1** — Isaiah's vision: "I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up."
- **Psalm 45:6** — "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever" (cited in Hebrews 1:8, applied to the Son).
- **Psalm 47:8** — "God reigns over the nations; God sits on his holy throne."
- **Psalm 103:19** — "The LORD has established his throne in the heavens, and his kingdom rules over all."
- **1 Kings 22:19** — Micaiah sees "the LORD sitting on his throne, and all the host of heaven standing beside him."
- **Ezekiel 1:26–28** — The vision of the throne above the expanse, with a figure like a man upon it in glory.

#### 3.16.1 The Davidic Throne

- **2 Samuel 7:12–16** — God's covenant with David: "Your throne shall be established forever."
- **Isaiah 9:6–7** — "Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David."
- **Luke 1:32–33** — The angel to Mary: "The Lord God will give to him the throne of his father David … and of his kingdom there will be no end."

#### 3.16.2 Christ on the Throne

- **Matthew 19:28** — "When the Son of Man will sit on his glorious throne."
- **Matthew 25:31** — The Son of Man comes in glory and sits on his throne, judging the nations.
- **Acts 2:30–31** — Peter preaches that David foresaw Christ seated on his throne (citing Psalm 16).
- **Hebrews 1:3, 8** — The Son "sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high"; "Your throne, O God, is forever."
- **Hebrews 4:16** — Believers draw near with confidence to "the throne of grace."
- **Hebrews 12:2** — Jesus is seated "at the right hand of the throne of God."

#### 3.16.3 The Throne in Revelation

- **Revelation 4:2–11** — The throne in heaven with the One seated upon it; worship around the throne.
- **Revelation 5:6** — The Lamb stands between the throne and the elders.
- **Revelation 7:10, 17** — Salvation belongs to God and to the Lamb who are on the throne; the Lamb shepherds.
- **Revelation 20:11** — The great white throne and the final judgment.
- **Revelation 22:1, 3** — The river of life flows from the throne of God and the Lamb; "the throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it."

**Symbolism:** Divine sovereignty and rule; the Davidic covenant; Christ's session and kingship; the final judgment; the eternal state in which God and the Lamb reign together.

## 4 Symbolic Numbers in Scripture

Biblical numbers carry typological and structural significance throughout the canon. The following are the most prominent and their canonical associations.

### 4.1 Three (3)

- **General association:** Completeness; divine witness; resurrection.
- **Key references:**
  - The triune name of God: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14 (the consistent New Testament pattern, though the word "Trinity" does not appear).
  - Three visitors to Abraham — Genesis 18:2.
  - Jonah in the fish for three days and three nights — Jonah 1:17; cited as type of Christ's burial in Matthew 12:40.
  - Jesus' resurrection on the third day — Matthew 16:21; 17:23; 20:19; Luke 24:7, 46; 1 Corinthians 15:4.
  - Peter's three denials and threefold restoration — Matthew 26:34, 69–75; John 21:15–17.
  - Jesus' three prayers in Gethsemane — Matthew 26:36–46.
  - Paul's three missionary journeys (Acts 13–21, narrative pattern).
  - Faith, hope, and love — 1 Corinthians 13:13.
  - Three gifts of the magi [UNVERIFIED — the Bible does not state the number of magi; three is traditional inference from the three gifts in Matthew 2:11].

### 4.2 Seven (7)

- **General association:** Completion; perfection; divine fullness; covenant.
- **Key references:**
  - Seven days of creation and the Sabbath rest — Genesis 1:1–2:3.
  - Seven pairs of clean animals in the ark — Genesis 7:2–3.
  - Seven years of plenty and seven years of famine — Genesis 41.
  - Seven days of the Feast of Unleavened Bread — Exodus 12:15–20; Leviticus 23:6.
  - Seven lamps on the golden lampstand — Exodus 25:37.
  - Sevenfold sprinkling on the Day of Atonement — Leviticus 16:14, 19.
  - Seven priests and seven trumpets at Jericho — Joshua 6:4–20 (marching seven days; seven circuits on the seventh day).
  - Naaman washes seven times in the Jordan — 2 Kings 5:10–14.
  - Seventy times seven — Matthew 18:21–22 (indefinite perfection of forgiveness; the Greek can read "seventy-seven").
  - Seven deacons — Acts 6:3.
  - Seven churches, seven lampstands, seven stars — Revelation 1:4, 11–12, 20.

## 5 Symbolic Colours (Supplementary)

Colours in Scripture convey symbolic meaning, particularly in the tabernacle/temple context and apocalyptic visions. This section is supplementary and less exhaustive.

### 5.1 White

- **General association:** Purity; righteousness; victory; glory; resurrection.
- **Psalm 51:7** — "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow."
- **Isaiah 1:18** — "Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow."
- **Daniel 7:9** — The Ancient of Days' clothing "white as snow."
- **Revelation 3:4–5** — White garments for those who have not soiled their clothes.
- **Revelation 7:9, 13–14** — The multitude in white robes washed in the Lamb's blood.

### 5.2 Red / Scarlet

- **General association:** Sin; blood; sacrifice; judgment; atonement.
- **Isaiah 1:18** — Sins are "like scarlet" — crimson imagery for sin.
- **Leviticus 14:4–6** — Scarlet yarn used in the cleansing of the leper.
- **Numbers 19:6** — Scarlet yarn cast into the red heifer's burning for the water of purification.
- **Joshua 2:18, 21** — The scarlet cord as the sign of Rahab's deliverance (the colour of blood/life running through her window).
- **Hebrews 9:19** — Moses sprinkles the book and people with scarlet wool and hyssop.

### 5.3 Purple

- **General association:** Royalty; wealth; authority; priesthood.
- **Exodus 25:4** — Purple yarn among the contributions for the tabernacle.
- **Judges 8:26** — Purple garments of Midianite kings.
- **Esther 8:15** — Mordecai wears royal robes of blue and purple.
- **Daniel 5:7, 16, 29** — Purple garments promised to the one who interprets the writing on the wall.
- **Mark 15:17** — Jesus mockingly clothed in a purple robe before crucifixion.
- **John 19:2, 5** — The soldiers dress Jesus in a purple robe.
- **Revelation 17:4** — The harlot (Babylon) adorned in purple and scarlet.

### 5.4 Gold

- **General association:** Deity; glory; purity (refined); kingship; worth.
- **Exodus 25:11** — The ark is overlaid with pure gold.
- **Exodus 37** — Implements of the tabernacle made of gold.
- **Job 23:10** — "When he has tried me, I shall come out as gold."
- **1 Peter 1:7** — Faith more precious than gold, refined by fire.
- **Revelation 3:18** — Christ counsels Laodicea to buy from him "gold refined by fire."
- **Revelation 21:18** — The city is "pure gold, like clear glass."

## 6 Notes for the Parser Aide

### 6.1 Reference Granularity

- Where a symbol spans a long passage (e.g., the tabernacle in Exodus 25–40, the flood in Genesis 6–9), the parser should treat chapter ranges as legitimate references, not only individual verses.
- Where a specific verse is the locus classicus for a symbol (e.g., John 1:29 for "Lamb of God"), that verse should be weighted higher in search results or suggestions.

### 6.2 Typological Chains

Several symbols form explicit typological chains that the parser should recognise:
- **Lamb:** Passover → Isaiah 53 → John 1:29 → 1 Corinthians 5:7 → Revelation 5
- **Temple:** Tabernacle → Solomon's Temple → Jesus' body → the Church → New Jerusalem
- **Water:** Flood/Red Sea → wilderness water → living water (John 4, 7) → river of life (Revelation 22)
- **Bread:** Manna → Bread of the Presence → Jesus as bread of life → Lord's Supper

### 6.3 Inverted Symbols

A few symbols are used in sharply contrasting ways and the parser should be aware of both poles:
- **Lion** — Christ (Revelation 5:5) vs. Satan (1 Peter 5:8)
- **Serpent** — tempter/devil (Genesis 3; Revelation 12) vs. instrument of healing when lifted up (Numbers 21; John 3:14)
- **Vine** — true vine (John 15) vs. vine of the earth (Revelation 14)

### 6.4 Verses Flagged for Verification

The following cross-references are marked `[UNVERIFIED — check before use]` within this file:
- **Song of Songs → Christ and the Church** (Christological reading is a received interpretive tradition)
- **Matthew 16:18** interpretive debate (petros/petra)
- **2 Timothy 4:17** "rescued from the lion's mouth" (scholarly debate over literal vs. metaphorical)
- **Matthew 2:11** — number of magi (three is traditional inference)
- **Revelation 1:4** "seven Spirits of God" and Isaiah 11:2 correlation
- **Revelation 12:1** the twelve stars on the woman's crown
