# Style — Simple, Descriptive English

Extracted from [Style_content.md](../Style_content.md) §7 — an independent register
sweep. Selected and run on its own, never combined with the Clarity/Brevity/
Coherence/Voice/Diction genre sweeps or their antitheses — it has no opposite
toggle; it always checks for this one register.

*Paraphrased from the **ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English)** standard,
maintained by ASD (AeroSpace and Defence Industries Association of Europe).
**Copyright ASD** — used here only as a paraphrased summary of the rule
categories; no verbatim text from the standard is reproduced.*

§7.1–7.2 govern word choice; §7.3–7.6 govern word use; §7.7–7.11 govern
sentence construction; §7.12–7.17 are general composition rules; §7.18–7.20
are miscellaneous.

### 7.1 Use the approved technical term

Use the correct, established technical term for the context — never an
invented or borrowed synonym.

Example: "hydraulic fluid" (approved), not "hydraulic juice."

### 7.2 Prefer the short, familiar word

Choose the shortest, most easily understood technical term available.

Example: "use" rather than "utilize."

### 7.3 No compound verb constructions

Do not build complex verb phrases with auxiliary verbs.

Example: "Remove the panel" rather than "The panel should be removed."

### 7.4 Use the active voice

Example: "Tighten the bolt" rather than "The bolt should be tightened."

### 7.5 Use a verb to describe an action

Do not turn the action into a noun.

Example: "Inspect the seal" rather than "Do an inspection of the seal."

### 7.6 Approved verb forms only

Infinitive (base verb), imperative (commands), simple present, simple past,
simple future, and past participle used as an adjective — no other verb form.

Example: "Install the filter" (imperative) / "the installed filter" (past participle as adjective).

### 7.7 Short, clear sentences, no more than 20 words

Example: "Remove the four screws. Lift the cover away."

### 7.8 No contractions, no omitted words

Example: "Do not" rather than "Don't"; state the full subject and verb rather than eliding them.

### 7.9 Vertical lists for complex text

Break a complex instruction into a numbered or bulleted list rather than one
dense sentence.

### 7.10 Connecting words link related sentences

Example: "Remove the cover. Then disconnect the cable."

### 7.11 Use an article or demonstrative adjective where applicable

Example: "Remove the cover" rather than "Remove cover."

### 7.12 Separate descriptive context from instructions

State the background fact and the instruction as separate sentences, not
folded into one.

Example: "The panel is hot after operation. Wait ten minutes before you remove it."

### 7.13 One instruction per sentence

Example: "Remove the cover. Disconnect the cable." rather than "Remove the cover and disconnect the cable."

### 7.14 Write instructions in the imperative

Example: "Close the valve," not "The valve should be closed" or "You should close the valve."

### 7.15 Give information gradually

Introduce one new fact or step at a time, in the order the reader needs it.

### 7.16 Use key words for structure

Repeat the same term for the same concept throughout, rather than varying it
for elegance.

### 7.17 One topic per paragraph, no more than five sentences

Example: a five-sentence paragraph covering removal, cleaning, inspection, refitting, and torque — split into two paragraphs if a sixth step is needed.

### 7.18 No phrasal verbs

Example: "Remove" rather than "take off."

### 7.19 Consistent terminology

Use the same term for the same part or action every time it appears.

### 7.20 No Latin abbreviations

Example: "for example" rather than "e.g."; "that is" rather than "i.e."
